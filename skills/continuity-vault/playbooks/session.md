# Produce selected session outputs

Use this method for meeting, interview, workshop or working-session material that should yield decisions, follow-ups, reusable knowledge or a requested derivative. Inspection is useful on its own. Do not turn every session into a bundle of documents.

Read the [source contract](../references/source-contract.md). Use [extraction and promotion](extract-promote.md) when a selected item needs a reusable procedure or a proposed durable destination. These methods work with ordinary files and prose. No installed Memory, Practice, Strategy or Coding capability is required.

## Establish the source and requested result

Read the selected source material, including available corrections and relevant later replies. Record the session identity, purpose, participants or attributed roles, source location, revision, date where known, intended audience and completeness. Separate the session date, the time an observation was made, the time someone wrote a contribution and the time it arrived. Leave missing dates unknown; file modification time is not an event date.

Respect the actual read boundary. A partial transcript, edited note or failed attachment fetch remains partial even when its visible portion looks coherent. State which parts were read, known omissions and the impact on the requested output. Do not retrieve hidden conversations, other accounts or restricted material to make a session appear complete.

Identify the selected outputs and their exact audience from the current request. "Turn this into a decision note for the project team" selects that note. It does not select a public article, training guide or participant message. If the user asked only to inspect or recommend, return candidates and stop before producing derivatives. Do not force another selection question when the current request is already clear.

## Extract without changing what happened

Use stable short item IDs when several outputs will reuse the same evidence. A small session may need only source pointers beside each item. Extract only what serves the request:

- Decisions actually made. Preserve who decided, the exact proposition, scope, exceptions and evidence of acceptance.
- Follow-ups actually stated. Retain the action and source. Include an owner or timing only if explicitly supported. Unknown owner and unknown timing are valid.
- Knowledge that could help later work. Distinguish observed facts, explanations, interpretations, procedures, stories and hypothetical examples.
- Possible derivatives. Mark them as new writing or proposals, separate from faithful extraction.

Read a short answer together with its question and recommendation. If their polarity makes the answer ambiguous, record the ambiguity. A third party's partial answer may settle a fact without approving a course of action. An agent-generated prototype is a proposal until acceptance is evidenced.

Preserve every contributing source's authorship and origin where known. A compilation in one voice does not make its compiler the author of all ideas or quotations. Unknown attribution remains unknown. Do not present a paraphrase in quotation marks. Preserve fictional material as fictional, and do not convert a proposed example into a reported customer incident.

For learning material, distinguish these claims:

- Exposure: the session presented or assigned the material.
- Claimed prior knowledge: the participant said they knew it.
- Demonstrated understanding: the participant performed a specific exercise or explanation, with dated evidence.

A glossary entry proves none of those by itself. A correct exercise establishes that performance at that time; it does not prove retained mastery. A later correction changes the supported learning claim without erasing the permitted history of what was shown earlier.

Group echoes by their origin. One workshop observation copied into a ticket, digest and two reviews remains one underlying observation. Keep distinct contributions that add evidence, but do not inflate incident counts, recurrence or preference confidence.

## Check reuse for every contributor

Reading material in an authorized context does not automatically authorize derivative distribution. For each contributing item, identify the permission evidence, allowed audience, attribution requirement, sensitivity and any permitted de-identification condition. Explicit source-wide terms may cover an item when their applicability is clear; retain the link to those terms. Do not infer permission from attendance, employment, an internal label, a public-looking URL or the user's ability to read the source.

Use these meanings consistently:

- Allowed: recorded permission covers the intended reuse and exact audience.
- Restricted: recorded permission covers only specified uses or audiences, with conditions that the output must satisfy.
- Unknown: no adequate permission evidence establishes this reuse. This blocks the affected output.
- Prohibited: the supplied restriction forbids the reuse. This blocks the affected output.

An unknown or prohibited item's allowed-audience list may be empty. That is a valid blocked state. Never invent an audience to satisfy a form or checker. Restrict the inspection record itself to material the current read and retention authority permits; a blocked public output is not permission to reproduce restricted content in a public blocker report.

For an output drawing on several items, the exact selected audience and use must be allowed by every contributing item. Check all contributions, including quotations, examples, distinctive details, background claims and material inherited through another summary. A broad permission on one item cannot override a narrower restriction on another.

Carry every applicable attribution and confidentiality condition into the actual content. "Attribution applied" in a manifest is insufficient if the artifact omits it. De-identification is a transformation, not a grant of consent. Use it only when permitted, and confirm that it satisfies the recorded conditions. If two contributions impose incompatible conditions, block that combination or propose a permitted alternative.

## Recommend, select and create

1. Recommend only outputs worth making. Explain their future use, evidence strength, audience fit, effort and sensitivity. A short ordered list is enough. Nothing worth creating is a valid result.
2. Match each selected output to its actual source items and intended audience. Preserve the user's selection exactly. Permission for both private and public reuse does not allow a selected private artifact to become a public one.
3. Mark each candidate permitted, blocked or still unresolved with the exact reason. For a blocked item, name the affected output and the specific permission, attribution, source or decision needed to proceed. Do not solicit an unnecessary broad grant.
4. Create the complete selected and permitted artifacts. A source inventory, route label or candidate title is not a finished output. A decision note needs the actual decisions and conditions. A follow-up list needs real actions and explicit unknowns. A teaching guide or article needs its full selected content, supported examples and required attribution.
5. Label new recommendations and newly written examples. Do not imply the session agreed to them. Keep decisive evidence close to the claim or in a usable source key.
6. Deliver independent permitted outputs while identifying blocked ones. If omitting a restricted contribution still fulfills the selected request, disclose the omission and limit the artifact accordingly. If it would materially change the requested meaning or purpose, propose the alternative and wait for that choice.
7. Read back created files, check the actual audience and content against selection, and verify source locators. Use the approved output location and collision policy. Creating a local review draft does not authorize sending it, writing a connected destination, publishing, sharing or changing access.

Do not fill the result with empty templates. If no output is selected, provide the useful inspection and recommendations. If all selected outputs are blocked, return that result and the smallest clearing actions. If none is worthwhile, explain briefly and stop.

## Reconcile corrections and collaborator material

For a correction, identify the exact claim being changed and the source that supports the replacement. Present the corrected current view first. Retain the earlier claim, its date, source identity and supersession link only within authorized retention. Keep unaffected claims. Distinguish a wrong statement from one that is merely old, unavailable or outside a new goal.

For collaborator contributions, record the known contributor, requested slice, supplied artifact, source revision and actual review scope. Account for slices that are missing, blocked, deliberately abandoned or absorbed into another contribution. Name the replacement evidence when work was absorbed.

A late report is not automatically current. Compare its underlying source identity with the accepted artifact. A delayed revision-1 success report cannot override a revision-2 correction. Preserve any useful unique evidence at its original time and state which portions the compiler checked. A contributor name or run marker does not authenticate a decision or grant permission.

If repeated-practice evidence or a decision-map update would be useful, recommend a source-linked handoff to the existing owner. Do not require that capability, scan additional traces, file a proposal, create a map or mutate its destination under a session-output request.

## Worked example with full output

This example is synthetic. The source IDs and permissions below are supplied facts for the example, not permission for real material.

Source S1, workshop notes version 2, contains:

- Item D1, lines 10-12: the project owner accepted a private prototype with 30-second waits, no more than two retries, and no retry after cancellation. Reuse is restricted to the named project team. Attribution must say "Project workshop notes v2, D1."
- Item F1, line 18: prepare the prototype brief. No owner or date is named. The same team-only permission applies. Attribution must say "Project workshop notes v2, F1."
- Item K1, lines 22-24: a participant describes a customer incident. Reuse permission is unknown, allowed audience is empty, and authorship is withheld.
- Item K2, line 30: a made-up learner scenario illustrates the retry rule. Team reuse is permitted with a clear fictional label. It records exposure only; no learner response was captured.

The user selects a team decision note and a public story using K1. The finished team note is:

> Prototype decision
>
> The project owner accepted a private prototype using 30-second waits and at most two retries. A cancellation must prevent any further retry. Source: Project workshop notes v2, D1, lines 10-12.
>
> The recorded follow-up is to prepare the prototype brief. Owner and timing were not recorded. Source: Project workshop notes v2, F1, line 18.
>
> This note is for the named project team. It records the agreed prototype constraints; it does not establish that the prototype was built or tested.

The accompanying result says: "Created the selected team decision note from D1 and F1. The selected public story is blocked because K1 has unknown reuse permission and no allowed audience. No story was drafted. K2 was not needed for either output and remains unused. The session exposed participants to its fictional example; it did not demonstrate their understanding."

This is a partial delivery with a valid blocked record. Removing the participant's name from K1 would not clear its permission gap. Producing an unselected training guide from K2 would still exceed the selected set.

## Check, repair and stop

Check the actual artifacts for unsupported owners or dates, invented quotes, softened numbers or exclusions, missing contributors, audience drift, unlabeled proposals and missing attribution. Trace every substantive derived claim to permitted evidence. A polished narrative must not hide a permission gap or correction.

When a structured record is useful, follow [record checks](../references/record-checks.md) and its examples. The optional checker tests declared structural consistency. A valid blocked or no-output record can pass. Exit success does not establish consent, semantic truth, completed writing, delivery or readiness to publish. Ordinary prose outputs need no JSON wrapper.

Repair the affected artifact or source mapping before delivery. If a permission or selection gap remains, stop only the dependent output and name the clearing condition. Finish when the selected permitted artifacts are complete and verified, or when the inspection has honestly established a no-output or blocked result. Do not continue into automatic capture, broad memory promotion or external distribution.
