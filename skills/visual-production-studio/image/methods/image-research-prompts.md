# Ground an image prompt in sources

Research the facts the image needs, rather than collecting a general dossier on its subject.

1. Identify the required entities, appearance, event/date, geography, labels, quantities, units and structural relationships. Separate supplied facts, facts requiring verification and deliberately invented content.
2. Search authoritative sources for those facts. Use the date relevant to the scene and retain source links next to the facts they support. For current conditions, record when the data was valid. Where credible sources disagree, preserve that uncertainty instead of selecting a convenient number.
3. Build a fact inventory small enough to carry into the final prompt. This makes the prompt usable in an image tool without search. For an explicitly requested generation-time research workflow, name the retrieval target, time anchor and behavior when information cannot be found. A SEARCH label alone performs no retrieval.
4. Design the visual container around the information: region and map extent, chart encoding, hierarchy, required labels, image references and source line. Scientific appearance and verified anatomy are separate checks. Do not turn a plausible-looking molecule or map into evidence.
5. Write the prompt with the verified inventory intact. Preserve names, values and units across design alternatives. Label reconstruction or imagined futures where they could be mistaken for documented reality.

Deliver the complete prompt plus concise links supporting the facts used. If browsing is unavailable, use supplied sources and mark the missing verification; do not invent dates, data or brand claims. On rendered output, check labels and relationships independently of its visual polish.
