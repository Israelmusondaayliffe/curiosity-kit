# Comparison and discovery

Read [the evidence contract](../references/evidence-contract.md) and use [research.md](research.md) for collection. Load only the section that matches the request. These methods work with native search, permitted page reads or supplied evidence; neither requires a peer-discovery API or another skill.

## Compare named alternatives

1. Resolve each entity separately, including product edition, release/version, geography and intended use. Confirm that the alternatives solve the same problem before scoring them. Preserve the user's explicit list. Do not introduce extra competitors unless they help an open-ended request or are clearly labeled optional context.
2. Derive dimensions from the decision: capabilities, quality of output, setup, pricing/terms, compatibility, operating constraints, practitioner experience or another requested concern. Distinguish hard requirements from preferences. Ask only for a missing criterion that would materially change the recommendation; otherwise state the working assumption.
3. Give each entity equivalent targeting effort. Use its verified official domain/accounts and relevant dedicated plus independent discussion. Search the same decision questions for both. Equal effort does not require equal source counts; record an unavailable lane or sparse side rather than padding it with old material. Read current first-party claims and relevant firsthand tests at the same specificity.
4. Build an evidence-backed dimension matrix in the working notes. Each cell holds the observed fact, version/date, source and limitation, or "unknown." Do not turn absence of a mention into absence of a capability. Do not compare a released function on one side with an undated roadmap promise on the other without that distinction.
5. Check every numeric comparison before calculating a difference. Align units, denominators, workload, sample population, measurement method, time interval and product version. If two benchmarks use different tasks or sample sizes, describe each result and decline a winner on that metric. A vendor's claimed improvement relative to its own predecessor is not a head-to-head test. Price comparisons retain currency, billing period, included limits, mandatory add-ons and observation date; unsupported totals stay unknown.
6. Search the strongest contrary case for the apparent leader. Check whether endorsements are affiliates, sponsored tests or repeated source material. A popularity lead can describe sampled attention but cannot settle capability, reliability or fit.
7. Conclude conditionally: select A for a supported need, B for another, or explain why evidence cannot decide. State which unknown could reverse the choice and the smallest useful verification. Do not manufacture a balanced tie when evidence favors one option, or an overall winner when the dimensions are incomparable.

For a sample-only comparison, label it as such. If A has ten independent hands-on reports and B only one announcement, report that asymmetry before judging adoption or quality. A richer evidence footprint can justify higher confidence in A's observed behavior without proving B is worse.

## Discover candidates, then investigate them

Discovery has two separate products: a nomination list and an enriched shortlist. Say which one you have produced.

1. Define the field and inclusion tests from the request: relevant problem, audience, geography, stage, current availability, recent signal, exclusion criteria and requested number. A request for "new tools" needs a stated meaning of new: newly launched, newly updated, or new to the user. Preserve it throughout ranking.
2. Nominate broadly but within scope. For alternatives, combine topic + "competitors," "alternatives" and "vs" queries, plus category and use-case language. For open-domain discovery, inspect relevant current category discussions, primary release/repository activity and useful curated feeds. Use source-language queries when the field warrants them. Accept lowercase/non-Latin names; capitalization and mention frequency do not establish a real candidate.
3. Keep a candidate ledger with discovery source, proposed identity, fit reason and unresolved checks. Treat lists, snippets, aggregator clusters and AI-generated search summaries as leads. Resolve namesakes and aliases against actual owner/product pages before merging. Use deterministic tie-breaks such as verified fit then name, never network completion order.
4. Deduplicate brands, products and shared origin carefully. A company and its product may need separate entries if the question distinguishes them. Respect an explicit user-supplied competitors list instead of silently replacing it with search results. Keep excluded candidates with short reasons when the exclusion matters to the request.
5. Enrich every candidate that could appear in the final ranked shortlist. Read the relevant primary body, establish date/availability and inspect independent experience when the recommendation depends on real-world use. Check the user's hard requirements. Expand a promising sparse candidate's search once with verified aliases/category wording; if support remains inadequate, label provisional or exclude with the actual reason.
6. Rank the enriched candidates by demonstrated fit and evidence quality. Explain the decisive evidence and tradeoff for each. Separate nomination frequency from independent support. Do not fill a requested number with irrelevant, stale or unverified entries; return fewer or a valid empty shortlist and say which filter or source gap constrained it.
7. Deliver a usable shortlist with direct item links, what qualifies each candidate, date basis and limitations. If the user requested only nominations, keep it brief and label the lack of full enrichment. If they requested recommendations, do not stop after producing names.

If the request turns into hiring-based discovery, apply [hiring.md](hiring.md) before treating job postings as business signals. If a source is private, candidate labels and inferred fit retain that dependency; do not move them into a public shortlist merely by omitting the private citation.
