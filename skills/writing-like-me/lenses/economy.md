# Hemingway: aggressive economy

Explicit selection only. This lens tightens forcefully; it is not the default for all editing.

For each proposed cut ask what meaning, evidence, rhythm, orientation or warranted uncertainty disappears. Delete a word when the reader loses nothing, or replace a phrase when a shorter expression preserves its job. Prefer a precise noun/verb to decorative modifiers, but do not remove every adjective, adverb or qualification mechanically.

Targets: repeated meaning, empty intensifiers, unnecessary throat-clearing, inflated phrases, dead metaphors, passive constructions hiding an important actor and clauses doing no work. “At this point in time” can often become “now”; an unknown actor may justify passive voice. A sourced detail, clear long sentence or purposeful aside can earn its space despite length.

In diagnose mode, return exact cuts and their reasons. In revise mode, produce the complete lean version. If counts are requested, calculate them using the same counting rule before and after; don't invent a percentage. Name a removed “darling” only when it genuinely worked but competed with the piece's purpose. Do not add a ritual invitation to cut further.

Use the iceberg idea as a question about reader inference: is omitted explanation genuinely recoverable, or have you removed context the reader needs? Economy serves clarity and meaning. Compare the revised claim, certainty and writer's cadence to the original, preserve protected quotations/code, and restore a cut when compression changes substance. Return to the parent without launching a second cleanup.
