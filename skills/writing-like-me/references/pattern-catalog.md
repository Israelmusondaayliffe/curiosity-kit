# Forty-seven contextual pattern families

Reauthored diagnostic taxonomy from the pinned Writing Quality sources. Families 1–24 derive from the Wikipedia Signs of AI writing taxonomy; this adaptation retains CC BY-SA 4.0 terms. Families 25–47 trace to the additional sources in [provenance](provenance.md). Numbers preserve source traceability, not a score or authorship classifier. Every finding requires context and an actual consequence. Protected exact material remains unchanged.

| ID | What to inspect | Repair and exception |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Significance, legacy or broader-trend claims without substance | State the supported change and consequence; keep significance when evidence explains it |
| 2 | Media coverage or notability used instead of a relevant claim | Name the source and what it actually establishes; a documented coverage summary may be the task |
| 3 | Trailing “highlighting/reflecting/showcasing” clauses adding apparent analysis | Remove redundant interpretation or state a supported mechanism; keep meaningful participial clauses |
| 4 | Promotional adjectives in a neutral account | Replace puffery with actual characteristics; requested advertising can express supported enthusiasm |
| 5 | Anonymous experts, reports or critics | Identify the actual evidence or narrow/remove the claim; absence of a citation alone is not proof of fabrication |
| 6 | Formulaic challenges followed by automatic optimism | Describe the actual constraint and response; do not manufacture recovery or future success |
| 7 | Clusters of inflated stock vocabulary | Prefer a precise ordinary term; preserve literal technical language and the writer's evidenced choices |
| 8 | Elaborate substitutes for is/has | Use the simple relation unless the chosen verb carries distinct meaning |
| 9 | Repeated “not just X but Y” contrast formulas | State the supported point directly; retain an earned contrast that clarifies a real distinction |
| 10 | Groups of three padded for symmetry | Use the natural count; an actual triplet is fine |
| 11 | Renaming the same entity through successive synonyms | Repeat the clearest term; do not infer a generation mechanism from variation |
| 12 | “From X to Y” without a meaningful scale | List actual subjects or define the relationship; keep a real range |
| 13 | Dash punctuation overuse or conflict with current voice preference | Review editable prose and punctuation meaning; exact quotes/code stay intact and no universal author diagnosis follows |
| 14 | Bold used as decoration or emphasis everywhere | Retain informative hierarchy and remove visual noise; respect the requested format |
| 15 | Bold label/colon lists that repeat their own labels | Use prose or a lead-in that adds useful detail; genuine parallel data can stay a list |
| 16 | Heading capitalization inconsistent with the destination | Apply the actual style convention; capitalization is not authorship evidence |
| 17 | Decorative emoji where the voice/channel does not call for them | Remove decoration or follow an explicit preference; do not impose a personal ban on every writer |
| 18 | Quote typography inconsistent with house style | Preserve exact source typography; normalize only authorized surrounding prose |
| 19 | Chatbot offers, acknowledgments or delivery language pasted into the artifact | Remove assistant chatter unless it belongs in the requested correspondence itself |
| 20 | Generic knowledge-limit boilerplate replacing a real evidence boundary | State the specific unavailable fact/source and date when needed; honest uncertainty must remain |
| 21 | Excessive agreement or praise before substance | Address the actual point; preserve a sincere supported compliment when the communication needs it |
| 22 | Inflated filler such as “in order to” or “at this point in time” | Shorten when meaning survives; preserve temporal or rhetorical distinctions that matter |
| 23 | Multiple vague hedges weakening an otherwise supportable sentence | State precise uncertainty once; never strengthen beyond the evidence |
| 24 | Generic optimistic conclusions | End on an earned specific consequence, decision or open question; do not invent a favorable outcome |
| 25 | Presenting an established concept as a discovery or novelty | Describe what the person did with the idea; verify an actual novelty claim |
| 26 | Announced emotions without supplied feeling or earned context | Show the supplied event or preserve the writer's actual reaction; never manufacture feelings |
| 27 | Vague concessions used to perform balance | Name the actual benefit and limitation, or remove the empty concession |
| 28 | Rhetorical question openers that stall | Answer directly unless the question creates a useful real inquiry |
| 29 | Parenthetical qualifications that only imitate nuance | Keep meaningful qualification, promote important content or cut redundancy |
| 30 | Numbered-list inflation | Keep discrete parallel items; no padding to meet a satisfying number |
| 31 | Internal process/reasoning narration in public copy | Present conclusion, concise rationale or observable evidence; preserve instructions that genuinely explain a process |
| 32 | Repeated acknowledgment or prompt restatement | Answer or advance the subject; retain a necessary restatement in a formal response |
| 33 | “Interestingly/notably/surprisingly” repeatedly telling readers how to react | Supply the fact or its specific consequence; an occasional meaningful emphasis may fit the voice |
| 34 | Excessive headings and sections for a short piece | Merge unnecessary divisions; use navigation the reader actually needs |
| 35 | Uniform sentence/paragraph rhythm or mechanical symmetry | Let thought and source voice shape variation; do not add arbitrary fragments, opinions or disorder |
| 36 | “Worth reading/attention” without saying why | Name the concrete benefit or relevant finding; a casual endorsement can remain in its context |
| 37 | Abstract metaphor nouns obscuring a component or action | Name the actual mechanism; keep literal math, biology, engineering and other precise domain uses |
| 38 | A colon standing in for a grammatical connection | Write the relationship clearly; keep colons for lists, labels, quotations and valid syntax |
| 39 | Feeling-language replacing what something does | State the supported mechanism, actor or result; emotional writing can legitimately describe a supplied feeling |
| 40 | Dense sentences requiring backtracking | Split or reorder the thought while retaining clear characteristic cadence and necessary conditions |
| 41 | Passive voice hiding a relevant actor, adverbs supporting weak verbs, false agency | Name the actor/process or measured change when known; retain meaningful adverbs and passive voice when appropriate |
| 42 | Throat-clearing or performed candor/exclusivity | Start with the point; keep a genuine voice marker only when it carries context |
| 43 | Staged colon reveal making an ordinary fact theatrical | State the fact plainly; do not erase useful labels or grammatical colons |
| 44 | Fragment stacks manufacturing intensity | Recombine when the shape overwhelms meaning; preserve an earned source-backed fragment |
| 45 | Self-answered rhetorical setup or “plot twist” label | Deliver the answer without ceremony, unless the setup itself is an intended comic or narrative move |
| 46 | A detachable profound kicker after the argument ended | End at the strongest earned consequence; do not replace it with a prettier unsupported aphorism |
| 47 | Summary-recapping the piece or announcing the next section unnecessarily | Move on or end; retain an actual executive summary, instructional recap or navigation requirement |

## Use the catalog

Prioritize unsupported material, changed certainty, broken meaning and reader confusion before vocabulary. Inspect pattern density and function, not mere occurrence. The local scanner covers literal matches and selected structures only; it cannot evaluate every family. [Structural checks](structural-checks.md) cover the deeper source-aware pass.

For a found phrase, identify its actual job, test deletion or a precise substitute, then read the surrounding passage. A replacement that sounds different but means no more is not an improvement. Never supply a real-sounding statistic, motive, anecdote or citation to make the example concrete. Keep intentional irony, warranted qualification, precise vocabulary and recognizably human source-backed personality.
