# Produce a workshop people can use

Use the ordinary source/structure/write methods, including the local writing and explanation references. This branch adds audience and facilitation decisions; it does not replace the finished participant guide with an agenda.

## Choose an attainable outcome

Identify what participants will make or decide, their access and starting knowledge, and what evidence could show progress. Distinguish operating a tool from checking or editing its output. If participants cannot log in, give them complete supplied outputs and a comparable reasoning/revision task; observation or pairing can help but must not be the only fallback.

Use safe fictional or authorized material so participation does not require disclosure of a real client, business or personal situation. Make private reflection and voluntary sharing possible. Do not require account creation, paid access, public posting or uploads merely to complete the exercise.

## Author the participant path

Include the full brief or source excerpt, necessary definitions, a completed demonstration, concrete instructions, practice inputs, success conditions, feedback and a useful finish. A participant should be able to attempt the task without the private presenter notes.

Show a result before unpacking a complex prompt when that helps orient the reader. Explain why the example works, then introduce a flawed or ambiguous output that requires judgment. Show more than one defensible response where wording varies. A next attempt should change a meaningful condition, not ask the reader to copy the answer again.

For each access branch, specify its starting material, action and output, then rejoin at a shared check. A phone-only text editor or paper route can still produce a checked revision. Describe the narrower demonstrated ability honestly. Do not claim a no-tool participant has operated the tool.

## Write separate private presenter notes when requested

Tie notes to participant section/activity names. Include preparation, what to demonstrate or say, questions that reveal understanding, rationale for answers, likely confusion, a respectful response and a recovery action. Include the actual source portions, example texts (including any referenced alternatives and revisions) and checks needed to demonstrate and judge each referenced activity without the participant handout; add interpretation and repeat only those needed portions, not the whole handout.

Use observable transitions when useful: proceed once participants can locate a supported claim and repair an unsupported one. Add timings only when requested or necessary to meet an explicit duration. Do not import fixed pacing timestamps, slide decks, speaker-note bans, exercise-folder scaffolds or tooling commands from unrelated course-authoring systems.

Prepare for login failure, inaccessible visuals, output variation and conflicting answers. Have the supplied material ready as an honest fallback. If a live demonstration fails, say what failed and teach from the labeled prepared example; do not present that example as the live result.

## Check before delivery

Attempt every participant branch using only its stated inputs. Ensure the no-account branch requires an actual decision and revision. Check full example constraints and feedback explanations; never reward exact prose equality when several answers meet the job. Verify that privacy remains intact if the handout is separated from notes. A presenter self-check is not evidence that real participants succeeded.
