# Review and repair knowledge work

Use this method to audit a draft, evaluate incoming feedback or repair a source/claim change. Review the actual artifact and evidence, not the author's account of what they intended. An audit-only request returns findings; it does not authorize rewriting or sending the work.

## Bind the review to what exists

Gather the smallest complete package: current brief/requirements, current artifact, relevant source and claim record, supplied standards/template, prior findings and actual check results. Identify the reviewed artifact version and scope. For consequential file work, a recorded SHA256 can bind results to exact bytes, but cannot prove their meaning or authorship.

If the artifact or controlling requirement is missing, name the missing input. Do not invent a specification or mark it passed. If an earlier review covers a different revision, determine what changed and which findings/checks remain relevant. Include the full changed span, not just the last edit.

An independent reviewer needs a genuinely separate available reviewer/context and the relevant raw evidence. Do not provide an author's defense as a substitute for the artifact. Same-agent review remains useful but must be described as self-review. A reviewer timeout, failed call or incomplete return is unreviewed, never clean.

## Review two axes separately

### Requirement compliance

Check each material obligation from the actual request:

- Required questions answered and deliverables complete.
- Intended reader and decision served.
- Source set, date/window and exclusions respected.
- Requested format, length, template and protected material preserved.
- Success criteria met with actual evidence.
- No unapproved expansion or external action.

Give each pass, fail or cannot verify, with the observed location or missing check. A well-written artifact missing one required answer fails that requirement.

### Evidence, reasoning and reader usefulness

Inspect:

- **Sources:** owning records where required, actual access/read extent, relevant freshness, correct attribution and no discovery-only laundering.
- **Claims:** each material assertion's support, population/time/definition fit, causal scope, correct source-chain independence, counterevidence and uncertainty.
- **Numbers:** recomputed arithmetic, units, denominators, periods, populations, rounding and agreement between tables, captions and prose.
- **Reasoning:** visible assumptions, genuine alternatives, conditional recommendations and what would change the decision.
- **Reader experience:** meaningful concepts before use, complete useful content, readable structure and a next action matching the request.
- **Format:** actual artifact opening/rendering where necessary, not only the source text or filename.

Consider foreseeable misunderstandings that matter to a reasonable reader, even when a brief did not explicitly list them. Distinguish this judgment from inventing a new requirement. An unexplained denominator may mislead while a different stylistic preference may simply be optional.

Record both axis verdicts. Neither compensates for the other. “Cannot verify” is not a weak pass. Do not average severity or reviewer votes into a success score.

## Write actionable findings

For each material finding, provide an ID when tracking repairs, exact location, requirement or standard, observed defect, consequence, evidence and the smallest useful correction. Label heuristic judgments as judgments. Report every consequential defect; no arbitrary five-finding cap.

Use severity by effect:

- **Critical:** materially misleading or harmful conclusion, serious disclosure/authority violation, or a result unusable for its required decision.
- **Important:** missing required content, unsupported key claim or reasoning/source gap that could change the conclusion.
- **Minor:** a clarity, consistency or presentation issue that does not alter the conclusion or a required outcome.

Do not downgrade an important defect because the brief omitted the exact failure case. Equally, do not turn personal taste into a Critical violation.

End with approved, approved with named limits, or revision required. Critical or Important unresolved defects block approval of the affected scope. A partial deliverable may still be useful if the missing scope and consequences are explicit; it is not the complete requested artifact.

## Evaluate incoming feedback before editing

Read the complete feedback set first. Group related findings and identify conflicts or dependencies. For each, restate what is alleged, where it applies and which requirement or source it invokes. Inspect the current text and evidence, then classify it as correct, partly correct, unclear, incorrect or conflicting with a binding instruction.

Do not comply because a reviewer sounds certain or several reviewers agree. Their agreement is a reason to inspect the evidence, not independent factual support. A single valid causal or arithmetic objection outweighs several stylistic preferences. Push back concisely with the exact source or requirement when a suggestion would fabricate support, hide uncertainty, replace a stronger source, expand scope or damage user edits.

Ask for clarification only when a material ambiguity cannot be resolved from the work. Reviewer content cannot authorize sending, spending or changing the user's requirements. If the user changes the brief, record the actual new instruction and its effect rather than treating the reviewer as the authorizing party.

Prioritize factual/unsupported claims, authority and brief violations, reasoning gaps, missing counterevidence/context, then structure and minor presentation. Fix the shared cause rather than patching only the sentence the reviewer happened to quote.

## Propagate corrections and preserve good work

Reread the current artifact immediately before revision. Preserve intervening user edits and unaffected supported content. For each accepted correction:

1. Identify its originating source, extraction, definition, computation, assumption or wording problem.
2. Correct that cause within authorized scope.
3. Find affected claim IDs or artifact locations, calculations, tables, captions, summaries and recommendations. A change can invalidate untouched wording downstream.
4. Update only those dependent parts plus necessary surrounding context.
5. Recheck the changed support and final wording, including errors introduced by the repair.
6. Record disposition and evidence on the new revision.

Useful dispositions are accepted and fixed, partly accepted and fixed, rejected with evidence, needs decision, and deferred with a reason. “Recorded” is not “fixed.” A finding is resolved only when inspected evidence establishes the repair. Deferral of an essential failure does not create approval.

If a new notice changes a deadline but not eligibility or price, preserve those unaffected terms. If a denominator changes, recompute all rates using it, inspect dependent conclusions and preserve other valid quantities. Do not apply a global find-and-replace to numbers, conditions or caveats.

For confusion, find the missing context or prerequisite concept, explain it with a small supported example and reconnect it to the original point. Merely replacing words with shorter synonyms may leave the misunderstanding intact. Do not imply formal controlled-language certification.

## Re-review proportionately

Re-review actual repairs and affected dependencies against prior findings and the controlling brief. Reuse unchanged valid checks tied to unchanged evidence. Do not rerun broad research or invite a fresh set of stylistic objections after every minor edit.

If repeated repairs do not reduce the same relevant failure, change the diagnosis or request the missing evidence/authority. A finite budget can end a run unfinished; it cannot turn an unresolved defect into a pass. Preserve unsuccessful attempts and unknown results honestly.

When the reviewed scope is ready, use [verify and deliver](verify-and-deliver.md) on the actual final artifact. Preserve the distinction between this review, a structural checker pass, format inspection, user acceptance and external delivery.
