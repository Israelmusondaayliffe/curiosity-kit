# Visual craft: choices, construction and diagnosis

Load the sections needed for the current design problem. [Design](../playbooks/design.md) owns direction/selection; [reference-to-code](../playbooks/reference-to-code.md) owns extraction; [verify](../playbooks/verify.md) owns acceptance. The entry's authority governs every option here. These are useful design families and procedures, not a required house style or a blacklist of fonts, colors, layouts or effects.

## Start with the job and the visible hierarchy

Give the important content the most useful prominence, not automatically the largest decoration. Inspect the first view before reading the builder's explanation: name what it communicates, the first things that draw attention and any area whose purpose is unclear. Blur or squint at the render to expose competing visual masses, then return to full size to check actual readability. Both scales matter.

Group related labels, values and controls; put an action near its effect. Separate distinct decisions before adding borders. In task interfaces, location/current state/back or close paths should be clear. Search belongs where finding is a real job; a small single-purpose page need not acquire search to satisfy a checklist. Keep recognizable affordances unless a tested alternative is better.

When a page feels generic, diagnose the observable cause: every section has identical emphasis, the reference identity disappeared, content is nonspecific, repeated framing adds no meaning, or controls are hard to find. A card, gradient, system font, rounded button or centered layout is not itself a defect. Do not swap competent navigation or a working icon family merely to create novelty.

## Direction families and useful alternatives

Select for the audience, use scene, content and accepted references. The former selector names are optional external choices, not local dependencies: general frontend direction (`design-taste-frontend`), product critique (`gpt-taste`), distinct composition (`hallmark`), premium direction (`high-end-visual-design`), industrial/raw (`industrial-brutalist-ui`), restrained (`minimalist-ui`) and semantic/Stitch-oriented (`stitch-design-taste`). Their bodies are not bundled or assumed present. The following local methods are sufficient when none is available.

| Brief family | Useful bias | Practical tension to resolve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimal, Swiss, typography-led | Clear grid, few roles, substantial negative space, solid fields or subtle texture; a small opening can be confident | Restraint must preserve context and discoverability, not hide the task |
| Editorial, magazine, fashion | Display/body contrast, intentional offset, image-caption relationships, varied story lengths | Asymmetry needs a reading order and sensible narrow interpretation |
| Cinematic, atmospheric, luxury | Strong focal object, controlled light/grade, expansive image frame or deliberate full bleed | Atmosphere cannot erase text contrast, value or the primary action |
| Product, SaaS, finance, infrastructure | Real product framing, clear data/control hierarchy, useful density, precise labels | Trust comes from behavior and evidence; decorative dashboards do not supply proof |
| Studio, portfolio, agency | Decisive scale, project-led imagery, editorial or poster cadence | Show work and navigation before effects; variety still belongs to one identity |
| Shop or product detail | Product/crop accuracy, option and price hierarchy, unmistakable buying path | Detail and comparison must remain accessible; decoration cannot misrepresent the item |
| Industrial or raw | Exposed grid/structure, utilitarian typography, direct controls, material restraint | Raw appearance still needs focus, contrast and readable density |
| Organic or playful | Warm/earthy or bold color, friendly forms, illustrative material, flexible composition | Match the actual audience; playful feedback cannot delay repeated work |
| Art Deco, retro-tech or maximal | Deliberate historical/geometric/type vocabulary, richer pattern and layering | Use a coherent reference and retain content hierarchy; avoid a collage of unrelated tropes |

The same family can be light, dark or color-dominant depending on the use scene. Distinguish these choices instead of treating every new brief as the previous site recolored.

Choose system ingredients together rather than randomly: a bright paper field, deep charcoal world, committed studio color or quiet neutral material can support different briefs. Type character can be clean grotesk, refined grotesk, expressive display, compressed statement, editorial serif/sans or rational Swiss hierarchy. A section system can be modular, alternating editorial, poster-like storytelling, gallery-led, strict grid or asymmetric marketing. Test the combination with actual content: compressed display type may make a strong poster but a poor dense settings label; a gallery rhythm needs enough meaningful images to sustain it. These are alternatives, not required picks from every category.

## Composition and section rhythm

### Build the page sequence

Assign each section a job: orient, explain, demonstrate, compare, establish genuine proof, resolve uncertainty or enable action. Then vary only what helps that job: density, image/text proportion, alignment, scale, whitespace, group structure, background intensity and section height. Keep shared type, gutters, CTA meaning and media treatment coherent. A dense explanation often benefits from a calmer transition; a repeated list may intentionally keep an even rhythm.

Draw a tiny sequence map before polishing: section → job → dominant mass/anchor → density → background/media → next action. Inspect the complete page after local work. Repeated blocks can be the right pattern for comparable items; arbitrary variation can make comparison harder. Conversely, a story need not become uniform slabs because one component is easy to reuse.

These content packs are optional examples for a requested scope, never default image counts:

- Four-part landing story: value/hero → useful capabilities → available proof or demonstration → next action.
- Eight-part product story: opening → established trust context → capabilities → product demonstration → use cases → available evidence → actual plans/pricing when known → next action.
- Twelve-part detailed story: opening → trust context → capabilities → preview → problem/solution → benefits → workflow → verified results or integrations → evidence → plans → questions → action/footer.

Omit or replace an unsupported proof/pricing section through the approved brief. These examples do not authorize fabricated testimonials, logos or results, and are not a requirement to pad a shorter task.

### Composition menu

Choose a dominant arrangement for the actual content, and vary it only where the story benefits:

- Centered statement: one compact message with supporting action, effective when hierarchy is simple.
- Top-left lead with lower/right support: separates proposition from evidence without symmetrical columns.
- Bottom-left or bottom-right over image: uses a deliberate quiet image region and a contrast-safe text area.
- Centered low: places text in the lower part of an image to protect its focal subject.
- Caption third beside visual two thirds, or the inverse: useful for explanation and product scale; preserve meaningful column proportions.
- Stacked center: label if needed, headline, support, action; useful for a restrained closing or simple introduction.
- Off-grid editorial: measured offset or bleed against an underlying alignment system.
- Image-as-canvas: text occupies an intentionally clear part of the image rather than floating over its busy subject.
- Inline large typography: the headline itself organizes imagery and negative space.
- Curated scatter or polaroid grouping: communicates a collection, with intentional overlap and an unmistakable entry point.

Left-text/right-image remains valid when it is the best arrangement. Reversal alone is not a new concept. For a hero, choose scale deliberately: giant statement for an important opening, balanced editorial for explanation, or mini/quiet for confident restraint. Protect readability and action on a small laptop. A short display statement often fits a few lines, but approved content and language govern wrapping; do not shrink text or rewrite the message to hit a line quota.

### Background and CTA menu

Backgrounds can do structural work. Useful families include solid surface plus inline asset; paper/texture/grid; full-bleed imagery with controlled overlay; editorial side-image; image-as-canvas; flat field with a detail crop; palette-matched tonal gradient; strongly graded atmospheric photo; duotone image; radial vignette with product; fine-grain tonal surface; and a two-field color diptych. Choose image grade and text contrast together. A quiet brief may use solid surfaces throughout. An atmospheric brief need not retreat to white, but every material must preserve the content.

Choose CTA form by priority and context: filled button, outlined/ghost control, underlined action link, full-width action band, strong closing statement with a small clear action, or a caption action attached to a visual. Meaning and hierarchy stay consistent even when form varies. Multiple primary-looking buttons in one decision area compete; a secondary action should have secondary weight. Name what happens, using specific labels that fit the task.

### Concept spine and memorable detail

When a narrative metaphor genuinely helps, use it to make layout and assets reinforce one another:

- Artifact/collectible: specimen framing, object detail, provenance captions.
- Journey: directional progression, waypoints, visible next steps.
- Precision instrument: calibrated alignment, useful notation, tactile control relationships.
- Living system: branching/growth relationships grounded in content.
- Stage: controlled spotlight and audience/performer hierarchy.
- Archive/dossier: indexed entries, clear captions and evidence order.

A subtle second-read detail can strengthen recall: a disciplined asymmetric bleed, structural large numeral/punctuation, material contrast, narrow editorial rail or macro crop carrying brand color. It must aid meaning or scan order. None is mandatory; decorative pseudo-system labels are poor substitutes for a real concept.

### Component families and how to make them legible

| Family | Construction intent | Failure to inspect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Staggered square masonry | Stable media ratios with intentional vertical offset and a clear grouping axis | Random offsets or DOM order that disagrees with reading order |
| Cascading deck | Consistent depth/light and selected foreground item; preserve an accessible route to every item | Hidden required content, uncontrolled overlap, tiny controls |
| Expandable image slices | Clear active slice and discoverable expansion by keyboard/touch as well as hover | A static image that implies controls the implementation cannot operate |
| Gapless modular grid | Exact shared edges, a hierarchy of large and small modules, consistent crop logic | Accidental seams, crowded text or arbitrary span sizes |
| Polaroid arc | Curated rotation/overlap, visible subjects, deliberate lead image | A scrapbook pile with no entry point or reachable content |
| Editorial offset | One underlying alignment system with purposeful exceptions | Every element opting out of the grid |
| Product screen stack | Authentic product evidence showing a sequence or relationship | Invented dashboards, illegible UI or misleading states |
| Vertical rhythm lines | Subtle alignment/reference rails that reinforce grouping | Fine-line ornament competing with text |
| Quote wall or split quotation | Genuine sourced evidence with readable attribution and order | Fake identities, clipped quotes or controls that conceal proof |
| Metrics strip | Comparable verified values with units, time/scope and explanation | Decorative precision or unsupported claims |
| Layered crop frames | Consistent focal points, aspect ratios and frame hierarchy | Crops that remove the very feature being explained |
| Marquee or gallery strip | Actual relevant collection with readable/static access and motion control when needed | Endless repeated filler, inaccessible links or motion that hinders reading |

These are available ingredients. Do not select a fixed number of signatures or add motion because a static comp implies energy.

## Typography as structure

Treat size, weight, leading, tracking, width and contrast as one system. Choose roles before font names. A verified existing brand face usually wins over a fresh fashionable substitute; a platform system face is a legitimate choice. Test letters, numerals and scripts actually used by the product, including fallback rendering.

- Display type can use tighter leading/tracking to make a coherent shape; verify that letters, accents and neighboring lines do not collide. A starting illustration is responsive 2–4rem display type around 1.05 line-height and -0.02em tracking, not a rule for every font or language.
- Body text needs comfortable reading rhythm. Roughly 45–75 characters per line, often near 65, and about 1.5 line-height are useful starting points for prose. Dense controls, code and scripts with tall marks need their own treatment.
- Use weight to distinguish levels when more size would waste space. Medium/semi-bold roles can avoid the blunt choice between all regular and all bold.
- Keep label/caption hierarchy legible; making supporting text tiny to magnify a headline damages the whole composition. Small caps may need different tracking; do not assign one tracking value to every role.
- Use tabular numerals for changing or comparable figures. Use monospace where aligned data/code benefits, not merely to imply technical sophistication.
- Make heading structure semantic as well as visual. Do not pick an HTML level because its browser default looks convenient.
- Inspect real line breaks. Balanced/pretty wrapping can help where supported; hardcoded `<br>` choices need responsive/language checks. A deliberate line break differs from a stranded orphan caused by the wrong font or width.
- Let text and relevant spacing grow with rem/em and user size settings. Avoid fixed heights around multiline content. Preserve selection/copy for content.

For an observed type mismatch, check loaded font/weight, container width, line height and tracking in that order before changing every margin. A single font fallback can explain page-wide vertical drift.

## Geometry, spacing and optical alignment

Use project spacing tokens where they fit; derive a small coherent set from a reference where none exists. The relationship matters: within-group gaps are generally smaller than between-group gaps. Optical exceptions are allowed when justified; no universal 4/8-pixel scale can settle every image or typeface.

Use Grid for genuine two-dimensional alignment and Flex for linear arrangements, with content-driven wrapping/minimums. Constrain wide text regions and allow useful fluidity. Choose breakpoints where content needs them rather than copying device names. Apply open alignment/whitespace before adding another wrapper. A container is useful for grouping, interaction or material depth; a border around every nesting level usually hides the hierarchy.

For side-by-side comparison cards, align shared landmarks such as title, price, feature-list start and action. A footer action can use an auto margin within each card; do not force all prose into a clipped fixed-height slot. Variable-height editorial content need not imitate a comparison table. Inspect both short and long copies.

Mathematical centering can appear wrong: an arrow's visible mass, a play triangle and a letterform's side bearings differ. Small optical offsets may help after checking the actual rendered asset. Adjust the visual element without shrinking the control's usable hit area. For concentric rounded surfaces, an inner radius approximately equal to outer radius minus padding often aligns their curves; inspect it rather than applying it to unrelated shapes.

## Color, material, imagery and icons

Define roles: canvas, raised/recessed surfaces, strong/muted text, borders, primary/secondary action and semantic feedback. Choose contrast pairs, not isolated swatches. A restrained palette may have one accent; a data-rich product may legitimately need several semantic colors. Their meaning should remain consistent and have text/icon/pattern support beyond color.

Keep neutral temperature and image grading intentional. Warm and cool can contrast deliberately; accidental mixing looks unrelated. Dark mode needs its own surface hierarchy and legible text/media, not a mechanical inversion. Check meaningful logos and transparent assets against each background. Preserve accepted brand colors while adjusting surrounding surfaces or approved variants for contrast.

Use shadow offset, blur, tint and light direction consistently to explain layering. A tint can integrate shadow with a colored environment; a border or spacing alone may be clearer. Texture, grain, glass, brushed metal or matte effects are conditional material choices. A blurred surface must remain legible over the busiest underlying content and have a solid/high-contrast fallback where needed. Heavy effects carry performance cost. Motion/material variants live in [motion and mobile](motion-and-mobile.md).

Imagery must carry information, atmosphere or structure. Choose the subject, focal crop and relationship to text together. A macro product detail and a contextual view can serve different explanatory jobs; repeat crops when comparison calls for it. Keep stable dimensions/aspect ratios and reserve space during loading. Meaningful images need useful alternatives; purely decorative images should not clutter assistive reading. Do not treat a comp's fake UI as product evidence.

Icons need consistent stroke/fill weight, perceived size and alignment. Use the installed family or verified assets; replace only for a real fit/readability problem. Labels carry ambiguous actions. A distinctive icon metaphor that nobody recognizes is worse than a conventional one. Include legitimate project identity details such as a favicon where the requested surface needs them, without inventing branding or assets.

## Controls, feedback and wayfinding

Design state families with the control, not after the normal screenshot. Use semantic platform/house primitives before a new dependency. A native dialog still needs a meaningful name, correct initial focus, Escape/close behavior, inert background and logical focus return verified in the actual context; a library name is not an accessibility pass.

| Surface | Craft decisions and failure questions |
| --- | --- |
| Navigation | Clear current location, recognizable links, accessible collapsed behavior and a way back/out; no essential hover-only destinations |
| Buttons/links | Specific action label, discernible priority, visible focus, press/disabled/pending states; actual destinations rather than `#` placeholders |
| Forms | Persistent labels, appropriate input/keyboard hints, helpful validation timing, error near its field and preserved input after failure |
| Empty/result states | Explain what is empty and why; supply a relevant next step or reset where useful; distinguish no data from no filtered matches |
| Loading/partial states | Reserve expected layout, show what is available and what is pending, avoid duplicate submit; skeleton only when it clarifies the forthcoming content |
| Error/recovery | Say what failed and what can be done; avoid raw stack traces, vague blame or a success toast that conceals failure |
| Disclosure/tabs | Visible selected/expanded meaning, keyboard behavior, stable labels, content reachable without hover |
| Modal/side panel | Choose modality for the task, not as a default; preserve focus/scroll context and close paths; avoid trapping a simple edit in an unnecessary interruption |
| Cards/lists/tables | Group by real relationship; keep key identifiers and comparison fields available; express row/action boundaries clearly |
| Footer/metadata | Include useful navigation, actual title/description/share assets and required legitimate policy destinations when provided; never invent legal pages or consent requirements |

Status, completion, warning and error feedback are different jobs. Animate only if it helps; use the motion reference for a real need. Hover styling cannot be the only affordance. Repeated tasks should not wait through decoration, onboarding tours or interstitials. Preserve purposeful explanations for complex/safety-relevant work rather than blindly deleting all instructional text.

## Stress the design with plausible extremes

Map each rendered value to its source, type, known limit and optionality. Use the real data seam with synthetic fixtures, not hand-edited screenshot markup. Include one mixed worst-case set and relevant empty/single/boundary-volume states. Keep fixture labels conspicuous; reserved `example.com`, `example.org` or `.test` addresses prevent accidental real contacts.

| Category | Representative synthetic stress | Typical cause and appropriate response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Names and scripts | Long compound names, diacritics, CJK/RTL, emoji clusters, one-character or missing names | Tight clipped leading, naive string slicing, forced one-line rows; allow proper shaping/wrapping and safe fallbacks |
| Identifiers | Long plus-addressed email, deep URL, UUID, versioned filename | `min-width:auto`, unbreakable text or shrinkable siblings; use `min-width:0`, suitable wrapping, and preserve distinguishing suffix/full-value access |
| Labels/copy | Expanded translation, long status/tag, many tags, empty title, newline, long pasted description, literal `<script>` text | Fixed button widths, orphan punctuation, unchecked rendering; wrap or disclose appropriately and keep untrusted strings literal |
| Numbers | Zero/one, large/negative/missing values, locale changes, live 99→100 | Bad plural/format rules, `NaN`, precision leaks or jitter; format the domain value correctly and use tabular figures where useful |
| Collections | None, one, page-size boundary and boundary+1, duplicate names, large list | False counts, empty extra pages, unclear identity or slow rendering; preserve actual count semantics and use appropriate pagination/virtualization |
| Time | Now/future/old date, timezone boundary, missing epoch, very long duration | Wrong timezone/relative thresholds or placeholder timestamps; show genuine date/zone meaning with locale-aware formatting |
| Media | Missing/404, extreme panorama/portrait, transparent logo, slow load | Distortion, invisible subject or layout shift; dimension reservation, crop rules and honest fallbacks |
| State/environment | Loading/error/partial/all statuses/denied action, narrow sidebar, 320px, wide screen, 200% zoom/text, supported RTL/dark/touch | Assumptions concealed by a friendly demo; observe actual overflow, focus, reachability and reading order |

Choose overflow behavior per field. Wrap identification text where feasible; clamp a secondary preview only with a usable way to reveal it. A filename may need its extension/version preserved. Amounts, dates and consequential values must not become misleading ellipses. Do not hide actions to solve overflow.

For each failure, record the concrete value, observed break, location, likely cause and smallest correction. Keep an inferred CSS risk separate from an observed rendered defect. After repair, revisit both the stress fixture and normal content so a local fix does not degrade the ordinary case. Acceptance and hardware-specific qualification remain with verify.
