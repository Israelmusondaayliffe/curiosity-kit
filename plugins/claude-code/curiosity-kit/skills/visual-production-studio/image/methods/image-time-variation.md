# Change an image through time

Choose the temporal change actually requested: age, time of day, season, weather, historical era, decay, restoration, renewal, modernization or imagined future. Do not add other axes to fill a preset.

1. Inspect the subject, object or world and identify what must remain recognizable. Separate defining geometry or identity from features that the requested time change affects.
2. Build physically coherent cues. Age changes suitable anatomy or wear; seasons alter vegetation and sun angle; weather changes sky, surface wetness and atmosphere; decay follows exposure, use and material; restoration reverses selected damage without inventing a different object.
3. For a period change, align clothing, technology, architecture, signage and materials with the intended era. Source-ground factual historical claims where accuracy matters. Mark imagined futures and uncertain reconstruction as such.
4. Choose one image, matched before/after views or an ordered progression. A matched comparison may hold camera and composition to expose differences. A narrative can change action and setting purposefully while keeping continuity.
5. Write each prompt with the source, target time/state, retained anchors, altered cues and coherent lighting. If several images are intended, distinguish separate files from one comparison sheet.

Check chronology, recognizable continuity and consistency between light, weather, season and objects. Fictional aging is an interpretation, not a prediction of a real person's appearance. With images, compare actual drift rather than accepting the preservation sentence as evidence.
