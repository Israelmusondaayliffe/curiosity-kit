# Translate faithfully between illustration and photo

Carry the recognizable character, object or world into the requested medium. A supplied target-style reference controls its assigned treatment. Do not replace it with a generic realism recipe.

1. Inspect content and style references separately. Identify the shapes, proportions, expression, attitude, materials, color relationships, props, composition and world rules that make the subject recognizable.
2. Decide what medium translation changes and what it preserves. A stylized silhouette or impossible anatomy may be an identity anchor. Preserve it if the brief does, even while making light and materials physically readable.
3. For illustration to photo, translate marks, edges, flat fills and simplified depth into photographic material, light and space. Choose plausible texture and construction without normalizing the subject into an average real person or object. Preserve fictional premises and intentional proportions.
4. For photo to illustration, translate observed form and atmosphere into the target's line, shape, palette, paper or surface, texture and depth. Photographic pores and every cast shadow need not survive unchanged if the chosen medium simplifies them.
5. Treat unseen details conservatively. Make the smallest useful interpretation or offer a few faithful treatments if the direction is open. Do not force a pose, wardrobe, fashion context or camera change.
6. Write a complete prompt with content/style roles, recognition anchors and the target treatment. Review whether the proposed translation adds unwanted identity, anatomy or world changes.

With a rendered image, compare recognizability and medium treatment against the actual references. Describe meaningful drift without inventing a numerical fidelity score.
