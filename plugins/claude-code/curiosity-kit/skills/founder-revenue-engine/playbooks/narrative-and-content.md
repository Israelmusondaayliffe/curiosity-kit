# Narrative and content

Use for positioning, an offer explanation, a commercial narrative, founder LinkedIn drafts, hooks or a bounded distribution plan. Produce the complete requested copy. It must work without a Writing Quality, Brand, Sales or other personal skill installed.

## Establish the claim set

Read the intended audience, offer, existing customer evidence, founder's supplied views, available proof, objections, channel and desired response. A validated JSON shape is not a validated ICP or a proved result. If the audience or offer is unresolved in a way that changes the copy, name that choice or use [discovery and offer](discovery-and-offer.md). Do not reinterview a settled brief.

Separate customer wording, founder statements, assistant proposals and measured outcomes. If voice examples are supplied or explicitly selected, preserve their relevant rhythm, opinions and level of certainty. Do not create first-person experience, emotion, profanity, quirks or credentials to make a draft sound human. With no voice source, write clear, restrained prose.

Create only the claim notes the work needs. For an important claim, keep its supporting source ID, exact scope and limitation near the draft or in the existing evidence ledger. “Helped one client reduce editing time” without a number cannot become “cuts every agency's editing time by 50%.” A proposed result must remain proposed. A synthetic example is not a testimonial.

## Shape the commercial message

Build a coherent argument in the order the reader needs:

1. The specific situation and job or problem, using supported audience language.
2. The current alternative and its evidenced limitation, without inventing customer frustration.
3. The offer and mechanism: what the buyer receives and how it addresses the job.
4. Proof at its actual strength: a measured result, a demonstration, a described process or an explicitly unvalidated pilot.
5. Material tradeoffs, fit limits or an objection that affects the choice.
6. The appropriate next step, if one is requested or genuinely useful.

This is a reasoning sequence, not mandatory headings or a compulsory CTA. A one-line positioning statement can be enough. A fuller sales narrative can include a short claim/proof table, objection responses and a plain offer summary when requested.

Distinguish a product category from an audience benefit. “An AI platform” does not explain what the buyer gets. Choose a plain category the buyer understands and show why this offer suits this situation. The real alternative may be a spreadsheet or doing nothing. Do not fabricate a new category, false exclusivity or “only solution” status for drama.

If an objection reveals a missing capability, cost problem or different buyer, expose that decision instead of writing around it. Do not invent roadmap commitments, guarantees, availability, service investigations or promised follow-ups. Use a visible placeholder only for a fact the sender must supply. Remove it before authorized sending, or stop with the exact missing fact.

## Founder LinkedIn method

Use this path when the user requests a post, hooks or founder-led content. A generic LinkedIn request can be answered directly from a settled source brief; do not turn it into prospect research.

### Facts and angle

Extract the facts, actual experience, opinion, audience, purpose and requested number of outputs. Preserve exact numbers, comparison sets and timeframes. Research a claim about current consensus or product facts only when it matters and available sources can establish it. Do not infer consensus from a memorized list of common takes.

An evidence-tested angle may question an assumption, explain an overlooked cost or show a concrete method. Useful questions include:

| Angle family | Question to test, not a prewritten conclusion |
|---|---|
| Arbitrage | Is a cheaper or easier route actually supported for this situation? |
| Hidden cost | What overlooked time, money or effort does the evidence show? |
| Structural limit | What boundary prevents the claimed outcome? |
| Burden transfer | Who does the work after a process is called automated? |
| Exchange | What benefit is traded for what constraint? |
| Career trap | Does a supplied experience show a skill or role becoming less useful? |
| Inversion | Does a supported counterexample change the usual recommendation? |
| Process overhead | Which step adds cost without the promised benefit in the observed case? |
| Optimization | Is the measured proxy different from the intended outcome? |
| Coordination | Where do handoffs or misaligned decisions cause the observed problem? |
| Status | Is a stated preference about useful work or image, and can that be established? |
| Invisible labor | Which necessary human work is omitted from the visible result? |

Discard unsupported angles. Do not force contrarianism or assign “tail probabilities,” rarity scores or predicted virality. These questions generate hypotheses, not evidence.

### Hooks and complete bodies

For varied structures, read the relevant rows of [the 69-hook library](../references/hook-library.csv). Its original names identify structures, not endorsed claims. Apply each row's evidence prerequisites. Examples are synthetic. A first-person transformation, result, emotional reaction or exhaustive test hook requires real corresponding source material or explicit hypothetical framing.

Choose hooks that the body can fulfill. A curiosity gap should close with a useful answer, not an exaggerated promise. Use genuinely different angles when alternatives are requested. Respect the requested count; one complete post stays one, while an open request for options can receive a small useful selection. Do not produce a large menu that makes the user write the actual post.

Possible body structures:

- Story: supplied context, actual event, bounded insight and application.
- Teaching: useful question, complete steps/example, evidence and limits.
- Results: method, measured result and uncertainty, only when measured results exist.
- Proposal: the problem, proposed approach and what would test it, visibly unvalidated.
- Comparison: explicit criteria, each alternative's evidence and a scoped recommendation.

Use short readable paragraphs and concrete words. Preserve a necessary technical term rather than replace it with a vague synonym. Keep quotations, source titles, command flags, identifiers and numbers exact. Never run global punctuation substitution over protected text. The original dash-replacement and synthetic persona-scoring scripts are not part of this skill.

For each full option, include brief rationale or source notes only when useful to review. Do not append a compulsory “quality score,” AI-authorship verdict or fabricated performance forecast. A post's style cannot validate its factual claims.

## Choose distribution before multiplying content

When a distribution or launch-content plan is requested, state the buyer question the piece answers, the evidence/experience it adds, where that buyer can encounter it and the useful next step. Use the founder's actual access, time and channel constraints. One strong piece in a suitable channel can be more informative than many adaptations.

Distinguish reaching a buyer from delivering a product artifact. A downloadable report does not establish how prospects find or trust it. Do not default to search-first, publish-everywhere, fixed content ratios or platform half-life claims. Verify current format limits only when they affect the requested output. Native image/video/document tools are optional prerequisites for those media; a text plan does not count as generated media.

Content can invite relevant conversation without promising a lead count. Proposed publishing and replies remain drafts unless authorized. If feedback exists, connect it to the next content or offer decision rather than treating likes as revenue. Use [outreach and learning](outreach-and-learning.md) for a bounded experiment.

## Final review and return

Read [evidence and review](../references/evidence-and-review.md). Check every customer-facing line, including hook, caption, CTA and suggested message. Verify exact counts, source attribution, scope, chronology, uncertainty and any promise. Make sure the body delivers the hook's useful point; remove manufactured FOMO, scarcity, proof, consensus or intimacy.

Return the complete requested draft with audience/channel context and only material caveats. If important proof is missing, use an honest process/pilot statement or withhold the unsupported line. Do not label an unfinished outline as finished content or a polished draft as market validation.
