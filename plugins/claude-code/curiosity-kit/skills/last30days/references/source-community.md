# Community and review sources

Use only the sections relevant to the question. These are collection methods for available native search, supported connected reads, permitted browsing or supplied exports. Source names do not claim installed collectors, authenticated access or complete platform coverage. Use [the evidence contract](evidence-contract.md) for common date/read/lineage/claim rules and [research.md](../playbooks/research.md) for synthesis. Keep private material and its derived queries out of public search without applicable authority.

## Find the right discussion

Verify the subject's official accounts and dedicated communities through attributable links, then identify broader category communities where alternatives and critical experience appear. Keep those two scopes distinguishable. A fan/support community can be highly relevant without representing all users; a broad community can confuse similarly named products. Select threads for topical relevance and substantive experience, then use observed activity to prioritize reading. Do not impose a minimum popularity floor that discards a small dedicated community's useful report.

Use short source-appropriate query variants: exact entity, entity plus use case/review/comparison, and specific problems or bugs when relevant. When narrowing loses recall, shorten the wording while keeping a verified entity/category anchor. Judge returned material against the full original question. Dates from search filters are discovery hints; check the post/comment itself and both bounds of the requested interval.

## Reddit

1. Inspect the verified dedicated subreddit with relevant new, hot or top listings when available. Choose the listing period consciously. Search broader category communities and global indexed discussion for alternatives and contrasting use cases. Topicless discovery can use a requested broad feed such as r/all, rising or top-week, but each retained item still needs the selected date/entity checks.
2. Use accessible RSS/search/listing pages as discovery alternatives when one route fails. An RSS updated date is not automatically the post's creation date. A search result's absence from one feed says nothing about other feeds. Record which routes actually ran.
3. Open candidate threads and read the post plus relevant reply context. Inspect top comments for common reactions and newer/contrary replies for qualifications, follow-ups or fixes. Keep comment author, date, permalink or thread-and-comment locator, parent context and whether the claimed experience is firsthand. Distinguish a question, joke, quote and an actual user report.
4. Deduplicate cross-posts/repeated links and shared-origin announcements. Independent commenters can supply distinct experiences, but agreement with the same untested claim is not independent factual verification. Preserve disagreement within a thread rather than assigning it one sentiment label.
5. Retain scores and comment counts only as observed, with time and source. Unknown is not zero; negative scores remain valid observations. A third-party archive's score is a snapshot from that archive, not a current Reddit count. If no archive is available, leave the missing metric alone.

No native/manual route promises exhaustive comments, archival backfill or API-sized result batches. State the selected thread/comment sample and any partial reads.

## X and other short-post networks

For X, create separate lanes when useful:

- FROM the verified person/company account: what they posted or announced.
- ABOUT the account/entity: other people's mentions, excluding the subject's own posts when assessing outside reaction.
- Topic/category discussion: relevant posts that may use an alias without tagging the account.

Start with the full entity/alias query, then a shorter phrase or anchored token if results are thin. Do not silently turn an ABOUT query into an author timeline. Inspect the post's full visible text, linked article/long-form note and necessary thread/replies before making a claim. Preserve original author, publication time, post URL, quoted-post relationship and observed likes/reposts/replies/quotes/views where available. A quoted or cross-posted announcement retains its original lineage. Indexed public results do not establish an authenticated timeline, full historical archive or exact engagement coverage.

Apply the same author-versus-mention separation to public Bluesky, Threads and Truth Social material when supported. For Bluesky use the original post creation date, not indexing time. On Threads a shorter keyword may improve recall, but reject off-topic matches using the original constraints. On Truth Social inspect dates yourself if the available search cannot bound them. Lack of an accessible post body is a source gap. Do not extract session cookies, app passwords, raw bearer tokens or browser storage to fill it.

## LinkedIn

Resolve the actual person or company through employer, biography and linked official identity. Matching a first/last name is insufficient for namesakes. Search public posts for the entity/topic and inspect the author's own posts separately from other people's commentary. If a relevant article appears on the profile, open its body and establish its own date instead of treating its headline as a full read. Preserve post/article differences, publication versus later edit, author role and commercial affiliation. Indexed public posts are a sample of accessible content, not all LinkedIn activity. Professional claims and self-reported outcomes require appropriate verification before becoming facts about effectiveness.

## Hacker News

Search the entity/category in titles and story bodies so Ask HN or Show HN discussions are not lost for lacking a title keyword. For requested broad discovery, inspect current or date-bounded feeds and check each item's date. Keep the HN discussion URL separate from its linked original article or project.

Read relevant comments and their parents, retaining comment-level URLs/IDs and authors. The most-commented story may be controversial rather than representative. Votes, reply totals and article metrics are different observations. Verify technical/factual claims at their original sources; a comment is direct evidence of that author's view or reported experience.

## Consumer reviews and Trustpilot

Verify the business domain and geographic/company identity before using reviews. Follow the user's explicit domain when supplied; do not pick a similarly named company because it has more reviews. Inspect individual accessible dated reviews and business responses when the question concerns recent customer experience. Distinguish verified-purchase labels, self-reported experience, moderation notices and the platform's aggregate rating.

A TrustScore or total review count is a retrieved aggregate snapshot. It is not a last-30-days rating unless the source explicitly provides that dated subset. A platform-generated AI summary is derivative material, not a sample of independently read reviews. If only an aggregate/summary is accessible, say that recent individual sentiment remains unverified. Do not trigger login/session harvesting or circumvent anti-bot restrictions. Use other permitted evidence and disclose the coverage gap.

## Deliver community evidence faithfully

Report recurring themes with concrete source support and meaningful contrary examples. Give counts only for a defined deduplicated sample. If summarizing sentiment, state who/what was sampled and how; do not imply population prevalence from search-ranked posts. Quote only text actually read and within applicable quotation limits. Paraphrases must preserve caveats and tone without inventing consensus. Keep primary announcements, firsthand experiences, secondhand repetition and promotional posts distinguishable in the claim ledger.
