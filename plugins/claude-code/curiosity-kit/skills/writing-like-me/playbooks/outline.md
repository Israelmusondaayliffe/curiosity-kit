# Outline

Arrange the supplied material into a useful shape while preserving the writer's intended claim or live question. Beats describe what happens in the piece: scene, observation, evidence, claim, question, objection, explanation or turn. Do not force setup-complication-lesson, a fixed beat count or equal sections.

Choose depth for the actual request:
- Light (the source's “10%”): controlling idea and major moves. Good for a short post or a writer who only wants a skeleton.
- Detailed (“30%”): the whole architecture in compressed form, not thirty percent of final word count. Good for long or evidence-heavy pieces and explicitly requested pre-draft review.

For a detailed outline, identify the opening's point and hook, its actual beats, thesis or governing question and reader promise. For each body section identify its main contribution, transition from the prior section, sequence of material, supporting sources and what it leaves established. For the ending identify what remains with the reader, the connection from the body and an earned final move. Use [the outline template](../assets/outline.md) only as an aid; omit irrelevant fields and create as many sections as the material needs.

Keep quotation/source references attached to their beats. Distinguish supplied support from missing support. List open loops such as an example needed, an unsupported connection, competing interpretations, an ending choice or an unresolved transition. Open loops are not excuses to invent facts or block all progress.

Check the architecture: are main and supporting ideas distinguishable? Does the sequence suit chronology, causality, argument or discovery? Does a section earn its space? Does an appealing aside contribute voice, context or evidence, even if it does not advance a tidy thesis? Preserve deliberate ambiguity and contradiction.

For a standalone outline request, present the useful structure and questions. For an already authorized complete draft, proceed when the shape is sufficient rather than inserting a new approval stage. If the user wants collaboration, incorporate their changes and stop where requested. The writer may choose a lighter outline or move directly to drafting.
