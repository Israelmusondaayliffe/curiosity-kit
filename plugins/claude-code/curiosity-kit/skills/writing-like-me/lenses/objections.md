# Fair-minded objections

Read as an outsider without earned trust in the writer or publication. Identify the actual claims and what must be true for them to hold. Confidence, familiar names and in-group shorthand are not evidence.

Test the strongest relevant objections:
- Factual: the claim may be wrong or only partly supported.
- Experiential: it fails under an important experience the piece ignores.
- Scope: it applies to a narrower audience or condition.
- Mechanism: the proposed causal/logical step is missing.
- Tradeoff: a cost or downside changes the decision.
- Alternative: another explanation or approach competes seriously.
- Moral: fairness, manipulation or values conflict with the case.
- Practical: time, resources, access or implementation makes it unrealistic.

Name which readers would object and why, rather than an empty “some people.” Ground each objection in a passage or explicit assumption. Do not invent contrary studies. Rate materiality: must address because it undermines the claim, can acknowledge because it limits the scope, or can omit because it does not affect the intended argument.

Steel-man rather than caricature. A good response may narrow the claim, state a condition, add actual support, concede a limit or leave a writer decision open. Do not force defense of a claim the evidence cannot carry. In diagnose mode, return the objection and possible response direction without rewriting. In authorized revision, repair only with supplied/verified support and preserve the writer's position unless a consequential reframe is approved.

Hostile read also attacks craft; this function concentrates on the claims, logic and evidence of a skeptic who is willing to be persuaded.
