# First-time reader

Read once from beginning to end before proposing fixes. Use the brief to know the intended audience, but judge what is actually on the page. With no audience named, use an intelligent interested first-time reader, not a detailed invented persona.

Track the reading sequence: what the opening promises, what the piece appears to be about, where terms or relationships need rereading, where setup arrives too late, where goodwill or knowledge is assumed, where tone creates resistance, where curiosity becomes confusion, where attention drops and whether the ending repays the promise. Later information does not erase an earlier stumble.

Distinguish these reactions:
- I do not understand: unclear term, reference, transition or logical step.
- I am missing something: necessary context is absent or late.
- I do not believe this yet: trust exceeds evidence.
- I do not know why I am here: relevance or direction is unclear.
- I feel pushed away: tone is presumptuous, condescending or exclusionary.
- I am losing interest: repetition, abstraction or pace interrupts engagement.
- I expected something else: the promised payoff disappeared.

Quote each material location in order, describe the reader effect before its editorial cause, and prioritize stops-the-read over stumbles over minor notices. Identify what lands immediately as well as what fails; no forced praise. Protect deliberate ambiguity and specialist knowledge appropriate to the intended reader. Recommend a kind of repair without replacing the text unless revision is requested.

This differs from Mom's supportive non-expert proxy and from objections' test of argument truth. It records the experience of following this piece. A cold summary and the most consequential remaining question can be useful; do not manufacture a lengthy persona monologue.
