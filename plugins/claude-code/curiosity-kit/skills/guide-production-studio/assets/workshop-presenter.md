# Private presenter notes: checked invitation workshop

Private presenter companion to the participant guide. All practice details and outputs are fictional authored illustrations. These notes contain facilitation guidance, not a record of an actual session or learner success. Keep them separate from the participant artifact.

## Prepare

Have the complete participant guide available in readable text and, if helpful, on paper. Confirm you can display the brief and examples without showing private tabs or accounts. Do not require attendees to provide business information, log in, pay, upload or post. A live tool demonstration is optional; the supplied examples support the whole check-and-revise task.

The outcome is a 60–90 word invitation, factual check and meaningful revision. People using the offline branch demonstrate source checking and editing. Do not say everyone has learned to operate an AI interface.

## Start with the information you have

Say: “The brief is our boundary for event facts. It tells us materials are included. It does not tell us about parking. Our job is to write something useful without turning a plausible detail into a promise.”

Ask participants to locate one supported detail and one missing detail. If someone confuses “unsupported” with “false,” say: “There may be parking. We just do not have evidence for that claim here.” Reconnect the distinction to the actual invitation rather than introducing a long abstract lecture.

Move to the example when participants can point to the words that support their chosen fact. If the term hand-building distracts, explain it as shaping clay by hand; avoid adding claims about the session's equipment or staff.

## See a complete invitation

Show the complete invitation before dissecting the prompt. Ask what the collection sentence does. It preserves the uncertainty instead of hiding it. The inquiry sentence also avoids inventing a booking process.

If asked whether the final access/parking sentence belongs, explain the judgment: it is an optional check, not an access promise. A shorter invitation could omit it. Distinguish a stylistic preference from a factual defect.

## Choose your working route

Offer existing-tool, text-editor and paper routes without ranking participants. If demonstrating live, identify the prompt and show the actual result. Keep a failed result for inspection if safe. If the tool is unavailable, say: “The live tool is unavailable, so we will use this prepared illustration to practice checking and revision.” Do not present the prepared text as the tool's response.

For phone readers, keep the source visible and work sentence by sentence. Pairing can be offered, but never make someone disclose a personal account or rely on a partner to finish.

## Find and repair an unsupported claim

Let participants inspect the flawed draft before revealing the feedback. Ask: “Which words in the brief support this sentence?” For “It sounds plausible, so can we keep it?”, respond: “It may be plausible. What evidence would let a reader rely on it? Until we have that, we can remove it or ask the studio. We do not need to call it false.”

The four repair targets are parking/refreshments, the following-Monday collection date, a guaranteed booking and broad easy-visit reassurance. A learner may find additional stylistic issues; keep the factual defects distinct. The complete repaired version retains every required event fact and the uncertain collection date.

If a participant deletes too much, ask them to check the required event details before adding new prose. If their draft is too long, remove repetition before removing price, time or location. If they change a fact while simplifying, return to the exact brief.

## Make a second attempt

The changed condition is to omit collection arrangements from the first invitation. Ask participants to produce an answer without copying the example, then show the answer for feedback. Check supported details and length; do not grade exact wording.

Two defensible approaches exist in the wider exercise: omit optional collection information, or explicitly state that its date is pending. Under this second task's specific instruction, omission is the appropriate choice. Use that distinction to show how task requirements and factual validity both matter.

If participants disagree between otherwise valid drafts, ask which reader need each serves. An energetic tone is not evidence of accuracy; a plain tone is not automatically better marketing. This workshop makes no conversion-performance claim.

## Check, revise and finish

Ask each participant to identify one revision and its reason privately or, if they choose, aloud. Observe the actual work rather than taking confidence as proof of success. Offer feedback on the specific sentence and next repair. The finish is a saved private invitation and fact check, not publication.

Optional extension: supply a new explicitly confirmed fact in a separate practice brief and ask which parts of the invitation and fact check should change. Do not silently alter the frozen original brief while grading an earlier attempt. Keep any actual session observations separate from these preparation notes, and obtain appropriate permission before recording or sharing participant work.
