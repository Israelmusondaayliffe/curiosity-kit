# Explain clearly to an intelligent adult

Use this complete local method while writing and revising guides. “ELI5” here means accessible explanation without assumed specialist knowledge; it does not mean talking to a child, reducing every subject to an analogy or deleting precision.

## Find the missing link

Identify what the reader needs to decide or do next. List only the concepts required for that action. Infer their starting knowledge from the request and supplied material, not from age, job title or presumed ability. When a missing prerequisite would change the whole artifact, ask one useful question; otherwise define the term and continue.

A reader can fail because a word is unfamiliar, a relationship is unstated, a mechanism is unclear, an action lacks a starting state or two valid choices are not distinguished. Diagnose which gap exists before making sentences shorter.

## Build the explanation around the current task

1. **Name the thing plainly.** Give a short definition using familiar words and introduce the technical term if the guide will use it. For an invitation exercise: “A source brief is the information you are allowed to rely on when writing the invitation.”
2. **Point to a concrete instance.** Connect the definition to actual supplied material: “The brief says materials are included, so that detail can appear in the invitation.” This is more useful than a second abstract definition.
3. **Show the mechanism or reason.** Explain the relationship the reader must understand: a fluent sentence can add a plausible detail that the brief never supplied; wording quality and factual support are different checks.
4. **Show a consequential contrast or boundary.** A detail can be unsupported without being false. A missing collection date permits omission or an honest pending statement, not an invented date. Use the distinction that changes the reader's action, not a generic contrast formula.
5. **Reconnect to the action.** Tell the reader how to use the concept now: locate the sentence's supporting source, or remove/qualify the detail. An explanation is complete enough when the reader can make the relevant decision, not when every related theory has been covered.

Use more depth when the mechanism needs it. Do not impose a one- or two-sentence limit on a complete guide, stop after each paragraph for approval or call a sibling method for essential “how” or “why.” A reference section can hold later depth while the main path supplies everything necessary to proceed.

## Choose examples and analogies carefully

Use the real task's objects before inventing an analogy. If an analogy reduces effort, state exactly what corresponds to what and where the comparison stops. Do not infer a factual mechanism from a metaphor. Avoid infantilizing examples when an adult's everyday work supplies a better one.

Prefer one stable example through the explanation when it exposes dependencies. Add a contrasting or ambiguous example when it changes judgment. Explain why two different outputs can both satisfy the same criteria. Do not replace a useful worked answer with a blank template or a slogan.

For a technical term, retain the exact term alongside the plain definition so the reader can recognize it in an interface or source. For uncertainty, say what is known, what is inferred and what was not checked. Do not remove cautious wording merely because it sounds less decisive.

## Check understanding without performing authority

Where practice serves the goal, ask the reader to explain a decision, predict an output, repair an example or apply the method to a changed condition. Give feedback that identifies the relationship, not just “correct.” Provide a complete answer or reasoned alternatives where independent study requires them. Copying the preceding example is not the same as unaided application. In a multiple-choice check, keep options similar in length and form and vary the correct position, so formatting cannot reveal the answer.

Do not force quizzes, public sharing or teach-back in every guide. A concise reference guide may need only a worked example and a lookup path. A workshop may benefit from a private check before voluntary discussion. Confidence or agreement is not evidence of mastery; only an actual response/attempt can establish what that reader demonstrated.

## Repair an explanation

Locate the earliest missing term or inference in the reader's path. Replace it with a concrete definition and example at that point. Show one intermediate step previously skipped, then reconnect to the original action. If the reader still cannot distinguish alternatives, use a case that differs in one relevant feature and explain that feature.

Reread the surrounding guide after repair. Ensure the new explanation uses consistent names, does not contradict later instructions and has not duplicated a rule without a new teaching job. Keep adult tone, the reader's objective and technical specificity intact. A model-generated prediction of confusion is a design clue, not proof of actual reader confusion.
