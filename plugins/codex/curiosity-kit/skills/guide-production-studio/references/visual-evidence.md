# Visuals that teach or substantiate

Choose a visual because the reader needs to recognize a state, compare alternatives, locate a control, understand a relationship or inspect evidence. Decorative images cannot substitute for a missing demonstration.

Before using an image, establish source, rights/permission, relevant version/date and intended audience. Remove private names, tabs, account details and identifying combinations only through an authorized process. A blurred area is not proof that all sensitive content is gone. Do not expose a private presenter screen in a participant artifact.

For each teaching visual:

- State the question it answers and place it beside the related action or explanation.
- Preserve enough surrounding context to orient the reader; crop irrelevant material without hiding a limitation.
- Use readable labels and a caption that directs attention to the meaningful difference.
- Supply text alternatives suited to the image's purpose. Do not rely on color alone to convey a required distinction.
- Cite or identify the evidence where needed. A mockup or generated image must be labeled as an illustration and cannot establish real interface behavior or performance.

To teach a process with three or more parts, prefer a short series where each diagram adds one part to the last over one diagram that shows everything at once.

When comparing outputs, use the actual comparable inputs and conditions. Explain what changed and what remains uncontrolled. Two attractive pictures do not demonstrate model superiority. When teaching a visual judgment, include inspectable rights-safe examples; prose that says “notice the difference” without the pictures is incomplete.

Inspect the actual delivered rendering at a usable size when tools permit. Check crop, legibility, placement, reading order, caption accuracy and whether the image still supports the stated conclusion. Record exactly which pages/screens were viewed. Automated link or source checks do not replace this inspection.

If rendering or evidence is unavailable, identify the specific unverified visual section. Deliver text-operable content with its limitation; do not invent screenshots or claim visual acceptance. If recognizing the visual is essential to the promised task, mark that task incomplete until the evidence can be supplied.
