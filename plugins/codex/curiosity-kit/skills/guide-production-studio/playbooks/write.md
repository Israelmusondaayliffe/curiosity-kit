# Write or revise the complete guide

Before writing or revising, read [clear-writing](../references/clear-writing.md) and [adult explanation](../references/explain.md). Apply both locally even when no companion skill is available. They govern meaning-preserving prose and teaching, not merely a final cosmetic pass.

## Write the artifact

1. Read the source boundary and reader path. Start with a concrete promise the artifact actually fulfills, its safe starting inputs and any material limitation. Avoid a long preamble about the importance of the topic.
2. Define necessary concepts through the current task. Give the plain meaning, a concrete instance and enough mechanism to explain a decision. Keep technical precision where the reader needs it; introduce the term rather than deleting the distinction.
3. Write the actual procedure. State where the reader starts, what they enter/change/do, what they should see or produce, and why that result matters. Include exact strings, paths or syntax only when supported and necessary. Do not substitute “configure appropriately” for the configuration the guide promises to teach.
4. Supply complete examples with their inputs and outputs. Real observed examples need their actual evidence and conditions; authored illustrations need a clear label at first use. Explain important choices and acceptable alternatives. Fiction protects practice privacy; it does not prove performance.
5. Make prompts independently usable. Include task, input boundary, relevant constraints, output needs and treatment of missing facts. A reusable blank is useful after a completed instance, not as a replacement for it. Keep code/prompt text uncorrupted by style edits.
6. Write the practice and feedback, if useful. Provide the full task, inputs, success conditions, a completed answer or critique and a next attempt. In a workshop, use its playbook for access branches and private presenter support. Do not leave exercise titles or “discuss this” in place of material.
7. Write troubleshooting from concrete symptoms: what differs, likely causes supported by evidence, the next diagnostic action, smallest repair and stopping/escalation point. “Try again,” “iterate” and “fact-check” alone do not teach recovery.
8. Add visuals only where they clarify recognition, comparison or evidence. Follow the visual reference; captions explain what to inspect and what cannot be inferred. Do not promise a screenshot or diagram that is absent from the delivered artifact.
9. Finish with the usable output and its check, then a compact lookup path if needed. Preserve the user's voice when supported, without copying private examples or importing unsupported claims from their older guides. Do not invent first-person experience, testimonials or biography. Do not add course, lesson or module framing to an ordinary guide unless the user asks for education packaging.
10. Run the local clarity and explanation passes on the complete text. Check that shortening did not remove prerequisites, reasons, distinctions, required facts or error handling. Review the artifact using [review](review.md), then deliver it in the requested format using [output-formats](../references/output-formats.md).

Complete the requested artifact in the current task. Do not impose paragraph approvals, mandatory interviews, a short conversational stopping point, diagrams by quota or a dependency on another writer. Ask when a consequential missing decision genuinely belongs to the user; continue unaffected sections meanwhile.

## Revise without losing the guide

Read the entire supplied artifact and the exact requested change. Preserve the original, legitimate user edits and unaffected content. Identify the smallest change that solves the reader's problem; do not rewrite the user's voice or architecture merely because a new version is possible. For uncertain or version-dependent facts in distinctive phrasing, preserve the recognizable voice by qualifying, minimally correcting or flagging the fact; remove the whole line only when requested or necessary for a material correction.

Trace dependencies before editing. A corrected date may occur in source notes, prompt inputs, worked output, answer rationale and quick reference. An access change may alter participant instructions, exercise input, expected ability and presenter recovery. Update all affected parts together, not just the introduction. Recheck unchanged facts before carrying them into rewritten sentences.

Rework explanation when confusion reveals a missing prerequisite: locate the first unexplained term or inference, anchor it in the current example, then reconnect to the action. Repeating the same abstract sentence more slowly is not a repair. Use the complete local writing and explanation methods on the revised passage.

Compare obligations by meaning, including within one sentence, bullet or section. Keep one owning statement per obligation and refer to it elsewhere when repetition has no distinct teaching purpose. Preserve specificity, distinct requirements and useful demonstrations. Do not flatten a worked example and a learner exercise into one merely because they teach the same rule.

After the targeted edit, reread the complete affected reader path. Recheck linked answers, examples, warnings, navigation and required visuals. A prior review applies to prior bytes; mark affected evidence stale until the changed artifact is inspected. Report what changed, why and the checks actually repeated. Preserve an observed failure rather than silently relabeling it a pass.
