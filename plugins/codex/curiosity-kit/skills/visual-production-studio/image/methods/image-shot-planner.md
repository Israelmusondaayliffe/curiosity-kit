# Make a usable shot list

A shot list is the deliverable. It can be complete without prompt writing or image generation.

1. Read the concept, reference, subject count, setting, treatment and required views. Establish what the set must reveal and how many frames were requested. Preserve constraints such as all close-ups, a fixed pose or no people.
2. Choose coherent coverage or exploratory directions from the brief. For coherent coverage, note the identity, wardrobe, construction, environment or lighting anchors that matter. For exploration, state what is deliberately allowed to change.
3. Give each shot an ID and a purpose. Record framing, camera position, subject action or static state, and the information revealed. Add reference needs, prop placement, light or lens only where the shot depends on them.
4. Select coverage appropriate to the subject. People may need establishing, portrait, expression, gesture, movement, relationship and detail views. Products may need silhouette, construction, use, scale, texture and label views. Groups need readable relationships and occlusion. Environments need geography, thresholds, depth, surfaces and lived activity.
5. Review the set against required views and the actual count. Remove duplicates that reveal nothing new, but keep intentional repetition for turnarounds or matched comparisons. Check that camera positions are feasible and shot-to-shot changes do not accidentally redesign the subject.

Deliver the list with compact continuity notes and any consequential unknown. Do not pad it to an old ten-shot preset. If prompts are also requested, expand each selected shot into an independently usable prompt while preserving IDs.

Use [coverage banks](../references/image-shot-planner/coverage-methods.md), the [full shot-list template](../references/image-shot-planner/shot-list-template.md) or the [preserved frame-plan format](../references/image-shot-planner/source-plan-format.md) as needed. These support people, products, groups and environments independently of any other studio.
