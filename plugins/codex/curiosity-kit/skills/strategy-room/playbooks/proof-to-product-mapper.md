# Proof-to-Product Mapper

Use when explicitly selected or routed to turn existing work, a repeated method or observed results into an evidence-appropriate reusable form. It supports guides, templates, workshops, services, internal systems, software, skills and plugins. Raw ideas may first need Compass; choosing who does the work belongs to Matcher. Read the [common evidence/choice contract](../references/evidence-and-choice.md); use the [productization brief](../assets/productization-brief.md).

## Grade what is actually proven

Identify the problem, beneficiary, repeated method, inputs, observed outcome, context and source for each material claim. Include failures, assistance needed and limits on transfer. Classify:
- **proven**: repeated evidence supports the result in the relevant context.
- **promising**: some evidence exists but a material uncertainty remains.
- **unproven**: primarily an idea or proposal.

Specify the claim/context being classified. A proven facilitated method may still have unproven independent use, demand or economics. A polished demo or repeated retelling is not repeatability. Do not invent adoption, revenue, differentiation or willingness to pay. Promising/unproven work can continue as validation rather than false product certainty.

## Compare three separate axes

1. **Delivery form**: guide, template, workshop, service, software tool, skill, plugin or operating system.
2. **Access**: internal, private client, member/community or public.
3. **Economics**: free, cost-recovery, paid, sponsored or undecided.

Compare plausible combinations against user need/context, proof strength, repeatability, customization, support/maintenance, distribution, rights/privacy, risk and effort to a useful minimum. Do not evaluate every theoretical combination. A guide or service can be the correct primary form; software is not inherently more mature. Public access does not imply paid economics. A private result cannot be republished without rights.

Inspect actual workarounds and behavior evidence when available. Check current alternatives only if they could materially change the form or validation path. Keep source-backed facts separate from distribution/pricing hypotheses. If rights or private inputs prevent a proposed form, narrow it or mark the condition rather than assuming permission.

## Map the smallest useful version

Recommend one primary choice on each axis plus one strong alternative and the condition that would favor it. Write intended user, bounded promise, required inputs, outputs, minimum useful scope, exclusions, evidence and limits, risks, support burden, distribution hypothesis and acceptance test. Preserve distinctive useful capability while removing delivery machinery that the outcome does not need.

For promising/unproven claims, propose the cheapest ethical test that could change the recommended form: a supervised versus independent-use comparison, a source-rights check, a real workflow trial or another claim-fit observation. Name the observable result, stopping/change condition and unknowns; do not pretend the test ran. A proposed price is a hypothesis, not a commitment.

Return proof classification with context, axis comparison, primary/alternative and complete brief/test. Check that the promise does not exceed proof, rights/privacy are explicit, and the smallest version remains useful. Stop before building, publishing, distributing, installing or selling.
