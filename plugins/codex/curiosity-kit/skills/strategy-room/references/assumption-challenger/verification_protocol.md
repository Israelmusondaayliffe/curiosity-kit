# Assumption Challenger: verification protocol

Load for verification/re-verification. Apply [shared evidence definitions](../evidence-and-choice.md) and use the [verified-findings template](../../assets/assumption-challenger/verified_findings_template.md). This is a deliberate reconsideration pass, not independent acceptance review or proof of truth. Verify every material finding that may reach the report in all effort modes.

## Preparation

Use the actual original input/version, raw findings, plan hypotheses, dossier, relevant source bodies/excerpts and access limits. Preserve IDs and versions. Re-verification can use an existing packet, but cannot treat inaccessible missing material as inspected. Identify new evidence or a changed claim and revisit the affected findings and dependencies, rather than silently rewriting the old result.

## Check 1: evidence support

For the exact wording of each finding:
1. Locate its cited D/S records and inspect the underlying relevant material where available. Does it assert the claimed fact, or only a related/weaker one?
2. Compare population, jurisdiction, units, time period, conditions and outcome definition. Does the claim turn one case into a population rule, adoption into effectiveness, willingness into payment, or documentation into tested behavior?
3. Examine method and source fit, not just tier. Is the sample selected? Are quantities supported by actual denominators? Does a source's expertise cover this claim? Is it an interested account? See [research_protocol.md](research_protocol.md).
4. Verify applicable currency and superseding material within available scope. An access date does not cure stale data; an unknown/future publication date is not evidence of freshness.
5. Check agreement and independence among cited items. Several summaries of one study do not supply several studies. If sources conflict, retain the conflict or show why one is more applicable.

**Repair:** narrow to the supported proposition, mark the missing premise, obtain decisive authorized evidence if useful, or remove the unsupported assertion. Synthetic example: a supplied survey records that respondents use newsletters; it does not establish that newsletters are necessary for business success. Retain prevalence only within the surveyed group if the methods support it.

## Check 2: inference and alternatives

Inspect the concise rationale and whether the conclusion follows from the evidence. Do not request a private reasoning transcript. Look for:
- Correlation or temporal sequence promoted to causation.
- Reference-class patterns promoted to a certain individual outcome.
- Failure elsewhere promoted to failure here without matching mechanism/conditions.
- Expert opinion promoted to fact without applicability or disagreement checks.
- An unspecified assumption filling the decisive link.
- A question framed to guarantee its own answer, or risk of action compared with a risk-free imaginary status quo.
- An absolute claim supported only by a conditional argument.

State the missing premise and whether it is independently supported. Consider the strongest plausible alternative explanation and what observation could distinguish it. A competing explanation may weaken causal certainty without erasing a well-supported descriptive finding. Do not downgrade every finding simply because alternatives are conceivable; judge whether they materially challenge the conclusion.

**Repair:** revise the claim to make the condition explicit, separate observation from interpretation, split distinct claims, or remove it when the core inference is untenable. Do not “repair” a gap by inventing a premise.

## Check 3: counter-evidence

Read relevant parts of the whole dossier, not only the challenger's citations. Inspect disagreement, null/negative results, temporal updates, different contexts and alternative mechanisms. Ask whether the challenge ignored the strongest evidence supporting the user's plan. Where the dossier's search was narrow, disclose that the absence of contrary evidence is inconclusive.

Weigh relevance and quality rather than counting votes. Strong applicable counter-evidence can overturn a finding; less applicable evidence may qualify it. When both apply to different conditions, split the claim or state the governing condition. Additional research must remain within authorized scope and be described as actually performed, not implied by the word “verified.”

## Disposition and confidence

The checks are **conjunctive**, never “two passes out of three.” A failed evidence or inference check prevents the original claim from being confirmed. The useful question is whether a narrower, explicitly hypothetical or corrected claim survives.

Record one status for each final disposition:
- **Confirmed:** the bounded claim is supported, its inference is sound and material counter-evidence has been addressed within stated scope.
- **Partially confirmed:** a specified narrower/qualified claim survives; the unsupported portion is explicitly rejected. This is not permission to keep a known flaw in the surviving wording.
- **Not confirmed by research:** the evidence available does not establish the asserted empirical claim. Do not publish it as fact; remove it or create a clearly marked conditional scenario if it remains useful.
- **Speculative:** a coherent what-if or proposed mechanism without direct support, explicitly conditional and useful for a test/contingency. Speculative does not rescue an incoherent or refuted claim.
- **Removed:** misleading, refuted, unrepairable, duplicate without extra value, outside scope, or generic enough to add no case-specific insight.

Use the canonical shared confidence definitions for the surviving claim, with a short basis. “High” does not follow automatically from one official source, a source count or imagined expert agreement. Low confidence still requires honest reasoning; a broken argument should be repaired or removed, not hidden behind a low label. Removed is a disposition, not a probability. Keep status and confidence distinct.

## Preserve the audit trail

For each ID retain original wording, original confidence, linked hypothesis, revised wording or removal, status, verified confidence, evidence check, inference check, counter-evidence check, source IDs, delta reason and a change condition. When splitting, give child IDs with the original parent reference; when merging duplicates, retain the mapping. Do not overwrite history so that an overstated raw claim appears to have been correct all along.

All raw findings must reconcile to dispositions, including null findings when their presence affects coverage. A synthetic set with one confirmed claim, one weakened claim, one speculative scenario and one removed assertion is a useful teaching shape, not a required distribution.

## Cross-lens and calibration review

For each convergence group, verify that findings actually share a causal/dependency issue, rather than similar language. Inspect all constituent claims under the three checks. Record common evidence origins and which consequences are genuinely distinct. A shared root issue can deserve high priority because it affects several decisions, while its evidence remains a single observation.

Inspect whether the challenger favored vivid, easy-to-source or negative material. Use [cognitive_biases.md](cognitive_biases.md) to test selective reading, generic critique, adversarial bias and confidence inflation in this analysis. Also watch the verifier's inclination to downgrade for appearances or retain an elegant finding despite contradictory evidence.

Track changes from draft to verified confidence with reasons. Many downgrades may indicate weak source fit or an overambitious plan; unchanged labels may be justified or may signal superficial checking. Counts alone establish neither. Recheck high-consequence and unexpectedly certain findings, not an arbitrary quota of random entries.

## Failure and handoff

If most findings fail, return the smaller supported set or request a tighter plan based on the demonstrated failure. If counter-evidence reverses the challenge's direction, state this prominently and let the report support the user's original plan. If required evidence is missing, identify the exact claim and smallest resolution; deliver only a bounded incomplete analysis meanwhile.

Before synthesis, ensure every material output finding has all three check outcomes, correct surviving wording, provenance, calibrated confidence and limitations. Removed findings remain available in the verification record but cannot be reintroduced as recommendations. Summarize systemic limitations, actual execution mode and any blocked checks. The synthesizer may simplify presentation, not strengthen the evidence.
