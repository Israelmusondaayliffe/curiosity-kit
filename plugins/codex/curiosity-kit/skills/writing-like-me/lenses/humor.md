# Sedaris: specificity and humor

Explicit selection selects craft analysis, not imitation of a living author's distinctive style. Find the writer's own supplied humor and material.

Look for exact detail, an honest self-deprecating admission, incongruous stakes/register, a sourced private thought, escalating events, an earlier detail that can recur with new meaning, or a buildup punctured by a real undercut. Specificity must come from supplied evidence; never invent the make of a car, a childhood scene, a private fear or an embarrassing reaction to make a joke.

Ask what the less flattering version actually was, if the writer wants to explore it. Preserve the writer's stated emotional register. A formal phrase placed beside an ordinary event can reveal a genuine contrast, but a pile of register collisions becomes performed voice.

Inspect timing: where a detail lands in a sentence/paragraph, what setup it needs, and whether explaining the joke kills it. A callback must gain meaning rather than merely advertise a designed ending. Avoid labels such as “hilariously” when the passage can carry the humor itself.

Return passage-specific opportunities, their mechanisms and tonal risks. A sample rewrite requires revision scope. Show alternatives only when requested or genuinely useful. Preserve the writer's factual boundaries and recognizable cadence. Tragedy, technical instructions or a high-trust message may call for restraint; acknowledge that the lens finds no appropriate comic move rather than forcing one.
