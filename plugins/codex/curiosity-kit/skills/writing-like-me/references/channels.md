# Destination and audience

The brief determines length, format, audience and number of outputs. Count final characters/words when a budget is specified; do not assume a platform's current limit or equate a word counter with platform accounting. Fit presentation to the destination without replacing the writer's identity.

## Business

Find who needs to understand, decide or act, and what evidence they need. A decision memo needs options/tradeoffs and an actual recommendation when authorized. A proposal needs the reader's problem, supported value and credible scope. An operating document needs usable ownership, sequence and exceptions. An informative update may simply inform; it need not sell or demand action. See [business and claims](../playbooks/business-and-claims.md).

## Articles and essays

Develop the whole requested piece. Supply sufficient context, a coherent argument or exploration, source-grounded examples and an ending earned by the body. An exploratory essay may leave a question open. Do not force a thesis, symmetrical sections or a neat transformation onto the material.

## LinkedIn

Deliver one complete post unless variants are requested. Use a specific opening, readable paragraphs, a substantive body and the writer's actual position. No invented anecdote, manufactured vulnerability, engagement bait, compulsory hashtag set or stock CTA. Fragments and short paragraphs can serve the material but are not required platform theater.

## X

Express the actual point within the requested format and budget. Do not replace meaning with a viral slogan. A supplied short-post brief normally needs no interview. A thread is a different scope: produce one only when requested or accepted.

## Explanation

Explain for an intelligent reader with the actual audience's knowledge. Define necessary unfamiliar terms, show mechanisms and relationships, and include sourced or labeled hypothetical examples. Simplification preserves nuance, necessary conditions and warranted uncertainty. Neutral explanation may need no personal anecdote or humor.

## Context profiles for cleanup

Use docs for procedures/README material (lists and precise terms welcome); technical-blog for technical prose (qualifications often necessary); investor-email for claims requiring particular evidence discipline; casual for light-touch replies; LinkedIn for requested social formatting; blog for ordinary long form. Profiles guide judgment, never override the brief, protect false claims or supply an automatic quality score. User punctuation/emoji preferences outrank historical fallback bans.
