# Interview the writer

Elicit the writer's thinking; do not decide what it means. The writer owns the claim, stakes, tension, framing and intended transformation. Capture their account before interpreting it. Keep confirmed words, open questions and model-proposed possibilities distinct.

Read existing task context first. Ask only about gaps that change the requested piece. Useful topics are the subject or live question; what the writer currently believes; specific events and evidence; their reason for caring; intended reader and baseline knowledge; and, only when persuasion is intended, what the reader should think or do differently. No fixed question count or mandatory thesis applies. Ask the smallest useful first batch: one clear question per item, without stacking required subquestions, and let the answers guide any follow-up.

Start neutrally: “What happened, specifically?”, “Which part do you want to stay with?”, “What feels unresolved?”, “What evidence is shaping that view?”, or “What words would you use?” Follow the writer's priority. If two statements might connect, ask whether they see that connection rather than supplying causality. Do not steer with praise such as calling a thread central, profound or revealing before the writer has defined its role.

Reflect what was actually said. In a standalone exploratory interview, offer a synthesis when useful rather than imposing one. In an authorized writing task, synthesize and continue without asking again unless the interpretation changes the writer's position. Proposed frames must be labeled, grounded and meaningfully distinct. Never turn an inference into a quotation, emotion, biography or confirmed intention.

Challenge a claim when the writer requests challenge or the accepted task calls for testing it. Ask what would change their view and what evidence supports or limits it. New/True/Useful is an optional claim test after a claim exists, not an interrogation standard for every personal essay. Preserve ambiguity when it is the subject.

Stop when the writer wants to move on or there is sufficient material for the next requested action. Return the confirmed subject/claim in their language, examples with provenance, audience where known, unresolved gaps and any explicitly requested proposed interpretations. Missing meaning stays an open question; do not delay a draft merely because an outline template is incomplete.
