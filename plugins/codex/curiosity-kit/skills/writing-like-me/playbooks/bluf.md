# BLUF: importance and placement

Diagnose where the important idea belongs. BLUF is not an order to put every thesis in sentence one. Read the whole supplied artifact, or the requested unit, before judging its opening. Establish reader, purpose, format and available attention. Default to an intelligent first-time reader when audience is unspecified; do not invent a persona.

Extract three distinct layers:
1. Declared: what the headline, opening or first beat tells the reader is important.
2. Delivered: what the entire piece actually supports, demonstrates or concludes.
3. Latent: a consequential idea later in the piece that might deserve prominence. The cleverest or most vivid line is not necessarily its center.

If several bottom lines compete, name the choices without taking ownership of the writer's thesis.

## Placement mode

Decision/recommendation/news/report: put the relevant conclusion, change or action immediately where the reader can use it. Essay/analysis/explainer: a hook can lead, but the opening movement should establish governing idea and stakes. Narrative/feature: a scene may carry tension before its larger meaning appears; delay stops working when curiosity becomes confusion. A short post can deliver an immediate point or earned setup/payoff. Resistant audiences may need trust or context before a persuasive claim. Instructions state purpose early then follow usable sequence. A paragraph or sentence normally exposes its contribution early unless a deliberate landing is better. No universal paragraph or word threshold applies.

## Five tests

- Stop-reading: at the natural preview (subject/first sentence, first post beat, headline/dek/opening, section start), what would the reader believe the piece is about? What important thing would they miss?
- Alignment: do headline, opening, body and ending agree about importance? A clear opening can still choose the wrong lede.
- Delay: inspect what precedes the delivered point. Preserve material providing comprehension, evidence, tension, trust, humor or purposeful discovery. Flag warm-up, repeated setup and background delivered before relevance.
- Hierarchy: does later material support, develop, qualify or apply the point in an order suited to the piece? Chronology, suspense and instruction can be better than descending importance.
- Compression: state the delivered center in one or two sentences. If that needs unrelated claims joined together, diagnose an unclear or split center rather than merely moving a sentence.

## Verdict and repair

Choose front and center; delayed but earned; buried; wrong lede; or unclear/split. Add intro drift, premature BLUF, local burial or repeated BLUF only when useful. Quote exact locations, identify the best position and name the smallest move. Protect the scene, qualification, joke or reader setup that earns its place.

Diagnose by default. In authorized revision, first try promoting an existing sentence, moving a paragraph, removing warm-up or tightening a bridge. Rewrite the opening only if rearrangement cannot solve the problem, and repair transitions created by the move. Do not add a stock “this article argues” label or silently change the central claim.

A hook generates attention; thesis work develops the claim; tracks removes revision residue; developmental editing reviews the whole argument; reader review records experienced friction; final pass assesses readiness. Use only the method needed.
